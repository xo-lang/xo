# Fast builds: where xo's self build spends its time, and stage 1 (2026-10-13)

fastbuild agent. Goal (owner, 2026-10-13): make Xo's builds fast, toward
Go's (make.bash: 46 s on r8g.8xlarge for three toolchain stages plus std
and cmd; bench/results/go-build-and-test-times-2026-10-13.md). Decision
0220 holds the plan; this file holds its measurements.

The build measured: `xo build --backend=arm64 --phase-times selfhost/xo`
(the self build of xo, 136,480 lines of Xo plus std), with a fresh build
directory (`XO_BUILD_DIR`) and `XO_BIN_CACHE=off`. The Mac: the shared
12 core development Mac (24 GB) under the heavy lock, other agents
running, `/usr/bin/time -l`, resident set and CPU time of the xo process
sampled each second with ps. Linux: the warm on demand r8g.8xlarge (32
vCPUs, Graviton4) of the lead's EC2 queue, GNU `time -v`, nothing else
running. One run each; timings on the Mac are noisy (other agents), so
the instruction counts are given too.

### The cc phase is a third xo, not cc

`cc -c` of the 437 MB assembly alone took 46.81 s (1.70 GB RSS) on the
Mac, and the one cc command that assembles and links it 47.91 s (a cc
wrapper's log, chain A's s1), so the link itself is about a second. The
other 64 s of the 112.41 s cc phase were xo's own CPU (the sampler: xo's

looked for each of about 50 runtime names with six prefixes, a
`contains` scan of the whole assembly each, twice per build (the link
inputs and the default linker's pack check). Fixed in stage 1 (one scan).

## Stage 1 changes (decision 0220 stage 1)

1. **Parallel emission** (selfhost/arm64/parallel.xo): the module's
   functions are cut into parts (contiguous runs of about equal SSA
   size, at least 128 functions, at most 32 parts); each part is
   generated by its own emitter on a task, `XO_EMIT_JOBS` at once
   (default the cores, at most 8); a part's string, position, and label
   names carry its namespace (`Lxo_p3_str7`); the reference counting,
   equality, box, and closure helpers are only named in the parts (by a
   hash of their key) and generated once in the last file. The parts do
   not depend on the job count, so the assembly does not either. A
   module of one part is generated as before.
2. **Parallel assembly**: each part is a file of its own (functions and
   trampolines are hidden global symbols), assembled by parallel `cc
   -c` processes (or selfhost/macho on tasks with `--as=macho`), then
   one link.
3. **One scan for the runtime names** (io_flags).
4. **insert_rc on tasks** after borrow inference (ssa insert_rc_jobs).
5. **The inlined module's verification on tasks** (ssa verify_all_jobs).

### What is left serial, and next

G on the Mac: the check, SSA, and early pass phases (3.69 s) and the
serial parts of insert_rc (borrow inference) and inlining (inline_module)
run on one core; the emit phase waits for its slowest part; then the
assembly and the link. Next, ranked by expected gain:

1. More jobs on large machines: the default is the cores up to 8; with
   `XO_EMIT_JOBS=16` the r8g.8xlarge build took 49.83 s against F's
   87.84 s at 8 (G also parallelizes the verification), at 19.86 GB peak
   RSS. A default by the machine's memory as well as its cores.
2. Smaller output: the spill and reference counting code above is most
   of the 22 million lines; fewer lines shrink emission, assembly, and
   the binary together.
3. `--as=macho` by default where it applies (2.82 s against 9.72 s).
4. inline_module itself on tasks (its callees' bodies are read as each
   is inlined, so the order matters; a snapshot of the callees would
   make it parallel at the cost of changing the inlined code).
5. The LLVM build: one clang job per core and more split modules.

What dominates: clang, 97 and 94 percent of the wall at the two settings.
xo's own share (about 67 s: insert_rc 41 s, emit 14 s, write 7.5 s, the front end 4.7 s) is
what stage 1's insert_rc_jobs already parallelizes (not yet in the seed
measured). The IR is 1.11 GB (2.5 times the arm64 assembly's 437 MB),
and clang compiles it at about 0.15 MB/s per process at -O2 and 0.65
MB/s at -O0. More modules and jobs cut the wall 2.2 times but raised the
total CPU 1.7 times: 32 clangs on 16 cores (two hyperthreads each) and
the copies of shared declarations in every module. -O1 saves only 10
percent over -O2; -O0 is 4.3 times faster than -O2 but its object is 2.3
times larger (and the code slower).

Against the target (the arm64 build's 88 s class on r8g.8xlarge): at
-O2 the clang CPU alone is about 11,200 s, at least 700 s on 16 cores
with perfect parallelism, so -O2 cannot reach it with this IR. Ranked
next steps for x86:

1. Smaller IR, the same lever as arm64's spill and reference counting
   lines: 1.11 GB for 6,103 functions is about 180 KB per function.
   Measure what the IR is made of (per instruction kind, as for the
   assembly above) and cut its largest source.
2. `-O0` or `-O1` for development self builds (the bootstrap and test
   compilers), -O2 for releases: -O0 alone would be about 1,700 s of
   clang CPU, about 110 s on 16 cores with 16 jobs.
3. Jobs by physical cores, not vCPUs (16 here), with splits a multiple
   of them, to stop the 32 way CPU inflation.
4. A native x86-64 emitter in the arm64 emitter's style (decision 0158),
   which takes clang out of the path entirely; the larger piece of work.

consecutive field drops from one base, 3.4 million drop calls and 8.6
million lines (38 percent), from only 370 distinct sequences: when a

(The last row's builds produce a different, smaller program than the
earlier rows', 17.0 million lines of assembly; compare within a row.)

The cause, found with perf on r8g.8xlarge (A and B building
acd142c7, 199 Hz): B spent 10 percent of its samples more in
`arm64.G.field_words` (1,370 samples against 301). Its code had grown to
5,057 lines: after each call that writes back the emitter's state
(a `var` parameter of about 300 words), it loaded and spilled every word
of the state to release its 200 heap leaves at once. The arm64 emitter
reads a call's `var` results in place while only field reads use them
(byref.xo `lazy_result`); a release of the whole result is not a field
read, so every word was loaded. `group_releases` no longer joins the
releases of a call's results as a whole (9dde75fd); ff12d428 joins
them one result at a time (a release of `Field(call, k)`), which keeps
the in place reads and the smaller output.

## Back-end cache stage A: objects per part (the Mac, 2026-10-14)

Decision 0220 addendum item 3 (accepted 2026-10-14). The parts of a split
arm64 assembly are cut by their contents (a cut after a function whose
name hashes to 0 modulo 128 once a part has 64 functions, at most 512),
their labels are named by the hash of the part's first function and
numbered from 0 within the part, and each part's object is cached under
<user cache>/xo/backend/obj by the SHA-256 of the assembler's identity
(cc: its `--version` and file, the flags, the source path; selfhost/macho:
the running xo's executable) and the part's assembly. An edit therefore
changes the assembly of the parts holding the changed functions only.

Steps (scratchpad measure.sh): `xo build --backend=arm64 --phase-times -o
OUT/xo selfhost/xo` from the worktree root, twice (warm), then after an
edit of one constant (selfhost/cli/core/fix.xo `MAX_ROUNDS = 25` to a
value not built before), then after adding one statement to a function
of that file (`if v == "<new>" { return Ok([]) }` in select_rewriters),
then on the reverted source. "base" is main dcdc040e's xo (no cache),
