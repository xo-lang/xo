# Borrowed projections (decision 0186), 2026-10-12

rc-borrow agent. `before` is the compiler at d819a82 (main before
decision 0186, an archive copy); `after` is this branch (d468930 and
later). Apple M4 Pro shared by about ten
agents (load averages 6 to 22): wall clock is noisy, counts are exact.

Retain and release counts are the operations an arm64 build executes:
measured with scratch copies of both compilers that call a counter in
the runtime before every `retain` and `release` the arm64 emitter emits
(after its own cancellation), printed at exit with `XO_LEAKCHECK=1`.
The instrumentation (scratchpad `instrument.py`) is not committed; the
timed binaries are built without it.

The first version (without rule 3) made last_long's caller 3
allocations dearer (47): gotchas/borrowed-projection-keeps-owner-alive.md.
In count_logging (checked in its rc stage) the body's `var` call
consumes `g`, so the block projection `g.blocks[b]` keeps its count, and
the element is borrowed from that block's list, which nothing in the
loop consumes.

## xo1 compiling selfhost/xo (the switch-over measurement)

As in xo1-perf-2026-10-12.md section 5, on this branch's sources (which
include selfhost/ssa borrow.xo): A_old is xo1 built by the `before` Go
compiler, A_new by the `after` one (`xo build --backend=arm64`); G is
`cd selfhost && xo asmdump xo` by the `after` Go compiler; B_old and
B_new are `A asm --phase-times --std=std selfhost/xo` under
`/usr/bin/time -l` with `XO_LEAKCHECK=1`. One run each, under the heavy
lock; load averages 10.3 (G), 5.0 (B_old), 5.2 (B_new) at their starts.

**The fixed point holds: G.s, B_old.s, and B_new.s are byte identical**
(sha256 37e493c0...7b66, 14,416,891 lines). So xo1's own rc stage
(selfhost/ssa) matches the Go pass on the whole compiler, and an xo1
built from B_new's assembly is A_new (C would equal B).

Retains and releases executed, from counting builds of the same xo1
(C_old, C_new: the counting compilers of the first section; their
assembly of selfhost/xo is the same sha256, 37e493c0...7b66):

| | C_old | C_new | change |
|---|---|---|---|
| retains | 6,312,795,692 | 4,277,594,041 | -32.2% |
| releases | 5,051,526,011 | 3,016,324,360 | -40.3% |
| allocations | 4,314,041,160 | 4,314,041,166 | +6 |

Findings:

- Two billion of each kind of count operation go (a third of the
  retains), with byte identical output and the same allocations (6 more
  of 4.3 billion; not traced, presumably a consume rule 3 does not see).
- Instructions fall 2.2% and cycles 4.1%, much less than the count
  operations. Not measured why; the hot loops of G.dce and the register
  allocator's liveness had already been rewritten in the source
  (xo1-perf section 4), and the 4.3 billion allocations are unchanged.
  A sampled profile of B_new against B_old (as xo1-perf section 3)
  would show where the remaining rc helper time is.
- Peak footprint rises 0.9 GB (5%), consistent with owners living to
  the last use of what they lend (the decision's stated cost); one run
  each on a shared machine, so treat it as indicative.
- The `before` Go compiler's build of xo1 (A_old, 698.9 s, 6,977.8
  billion instructions) against the `after` one's (A_new, 263.0 s,
  5,772.5 billion) ran at load averages 68 to 130 and 11 to 43
  respectively (the kernel time differs ninefold); not a usable
  comparison.
