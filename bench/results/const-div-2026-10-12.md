text). Machine: the shared 12 core Apple M series Mac (darwin arm64),
about ten agents running; every run through the heavy lock, one at a

The C path on main at 617bc37: dur_fmt 129,047,393 on arm64 and
118,545,078 on LLVM; the ratios in the reading below are against it.

Reading:

- (a) alone raises arm64's instructions (dur_fmt 1.158x to 1.250x of
  the C, time_fmt 0.209x to 0.236x) while its wall time falls (time_fmt
  8639 to 7601 us). `sdiv` is one instruction; the multiply high is
  `smulh`, `asr`, usually a sign fixup `add`, and a 64 bit constant of
  four `movz`/`movk` where it is not hoisted from a loop
  (gotchas/arm64-magic-division-retires-more.md). LLVM is unchanged:
  it already lowers this way.
- The quotient memo was per MIR block, and a division that may fault
  ends its SSA block, so `u % 10^9` and `u / 10^9` multiplied twice;
  inherited by dominated blocks it saves 1.9 million.
- (b) the masks in `digits_word` (`v & 0x7FFFFFFFFFFFFFFF`, `(w &
  0x7FFFFFFFFFFFFF) << 8`) let the value ranges prove the dividend not
  negative and the shift in range: the loop is 11 instructions a digit
  (12 with `sdiv` before, 14 with (a) alone). It helps both backends and
  is kept. With `sdiv` instead of (a) the same source is 145,820,545
  (1.130x): fewer instructions, more time (5579 against 5155 us,
  time_fmt 8854 against 6976 us).
- (b') placing each digit at its byte with a masked variable shift
  costs a checked `8 * k - 8` per call: worse.
- (c) stripping trailing zeros in four fixed steps (four magic
  constants and four remainders every time) is worse than the loop,
  which usually runs a few trips.
- (c') two digits a trip (`x % 100`, then `(p * 103) >> 10`) is the best
  on LLVM (1.063x) and worse on arm64 (1.242x): its two magic constants
  and three small ones are made on every call, and most fields are one
  or two digits.

## Correctness

- `TestSdivMagic` (internal/arm64): the multiplier and shift against Go's
  division for every divisor 3 to 1999, 4,000 random ones, and edge
  divisors (10^9, 10^18, 2^62 plus or minus 1, 2^63 - 1 and - 2), over
  0, plus or minus 1 and 2, MinInt, MaxInt, the multiples of the divisor
  next to the Int limits, and 128 random dividends each.
- Golden `const_div` (internal/llvm/testdata/golden, 1,564 output lines): the
  quotient and remainder of 46 edge dividends (0, plus or minus 1,
  MinInt, MaxInt, 2^62 neighbours, multiples of the divisors) by 33
  divisors (1, -1, 2, -2, 3, -3, 5, 7, -7, 10, -10, 60, 100, -100, 1000,
  3600, 10^9, -10^9, 10^18, 4, -4, 1024, -1024, 2^62, -2^62, 2^62 + 1,
  -(2^62 + 1), 2^62 - 1, 6700417, MaxInt, -MaxInt, MaxInt - 1, MinInt),
  together and alone, a dividend the bounds prove not negative, and a
  digit loop: identical on the Go backend, arm64 (TestGoldenRun), and
  LLVM (TestGolden). A division by a constant -1 keeps its overflow
  check (the minimum stays out of the golden; the fault goldens cover
  it).
- text_table: identical on arm64 and LLVM with the final text.xo.
- TestSelfhostArm64 (`XO_SELFHOST_DIRS=const_div,text_table,perf/text,native_display`,
  the go backend): 4 runs byte identical, 265 functions identical.
  TestSelfhostSSA (`const_div,text_table`): 169 functions identical.
- `go test -short` over the repository.
