# fold_copy's SWAR loop, 20 to 25% of its instructions: 16 to 18% off case mapping, 2026-10-24

`obj.fold_copy` copies a span while folding its ASCII case, eight bytes at
a time. The loop was written for clarity and paid for it: the case was
tested per word with a compare and a select, the 0x20 per letter was
built with a shift, a mask and a multiply, and the "not a high byte" test
repeated an AND that `between8` had already done.

## The change

std/native/llrt/obj.xo:

- The case is chosen once, outside the loops: `fold_copy_lo` adds and
  `fold_copy_up` subtracts, where one loop tested `mode` per word. A word
  with no letters adds zero, so neither loop needs the `letters != 0`
  branch either.
- The 0x20 per letter is `letters.shr_logical(2)`: a letter bit is a
  byte's bit 7, the shift is logical so the top byte is not sign extended,
  and one instruction replaces the shift, mask and multiply.
- `letters` is `between8(w & F_LOW7S, under, over) & (w ^ -1)`.
  `between8` already masks with `F_HIGHS`, so `w & F_HIGHS` was redundant,
  and `b & (w ^ -1)` is the and-not the compiler can emit.

The tail loop (fewer than eight bytes) is unchanged in what it does and
loses the per-byte `mode` test the same way.

`xo bench --ab`, 10 rounds, the same program source built by each xo
(`bench/perf/unicode`), A is the base. B/A is what B runs over A, so
below 1.0 is better.

The ASCII rows are the loop itself and drop by 16 to 18%; the mixed rows
are mostly UTF-8 decoding and case tables, where the same saving is 1 to
2% of the whole workload. Every checksum is identical to the base's on
both sides (lower 692262292, upper 466604236, lower_u 830427877, upper_u
152392224), and the corpus and wasm runs below agree on every backend.

## Coverage

The `strings`, `runes`, `equalfold`, `strconv` and `template` corpora on
arm64 and llvm, the wasm goldens (TestSelfhostWasm), and the full fixed
point (bootstrap s2 = s3), since obj.xo is in the fixed point set.
