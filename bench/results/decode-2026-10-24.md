# A two-byte fast path in raw.decode: no, 2026-10-24

The question was whether a 2-byte fast path in `raw.decode` (the
`utf8.DecodeRune` of std/native/llrt/raw.xo, which sets up the
continuation bounds, then loops over up to three continuation bytes)
would pay for the callers that run over non-ASCII text. Profiled first,
and the answer is no: JSON decoding never reaches `raw.decode` at all,
and the regexp engine spends 4.5% of its time there.

## Workloads

Two programs, A = Xo, B = Go (encoding/json and regexp, Go's own
libraries), 20,000 iterations, same checksum lines, `xo bench --ab` with
5 rounds, both built from one revision.

- json: a 40 element array of objects whose names, notes and tags are
  Greek and Cyrillic with some accented Latin, decoded into records.
- re: `[\p{L}]+` over a prose text of the same scripts, `find_all`.

| workload | Xo | Go | B/A |
|---|---|---|---|
| json decode | 6,623,311,756 | 18,203,802,116 | 2.748 |
| regexp find_all | 11,926,789,506 | 12,532,481,263 | 1.051 |

B/A is Go over Xo, so 2.748 means Xo runs 2.7 times fewer instructions
than Go's decoder, and 1.051 means Xo runs 5% fewer than Go's regexp.

## The profiles

JSON, 53 samples at 1 kHz: no `raw.decode` frame appears anywhere. The
runes of a decoded string are validated instead, and that is 34% of the
run: `scan.utf8_valid` 18.9% flat (37.7% cumulative, calling
`scan.seq_len` from `jsontext.str_at` through `llrt_json_str`),
`scan.seq_len` 15.1% flat, `jsontext.scan` 11.3%.

Regexp, 134 samples at 500 Hz: `raw.decode` 4.5% flat, behind
`regexp.backtrack` at 88.1% cumulative and `obj.rl_new_n` 7.5%.

## What it means

No change. A 2-byte fast path in `raw.decode` could move at most a
twentieth of the regexp row and nothing of the JSON row, and the regexp
row is already 5% under Go.

The next thing the profile does point at, if someone wants it, is
`scan.seq_len`'s own ladder for the same reason: on text that is mostly
2-byte runes it checks the continuation bounds, the lead byte range and
then loops, where a 2-byte case (lead `0xC2` to `0xDF`, one continuation
byte in `0x80` to `0xBF`) could answer at once. That would go at UTF-8
validation, which JSON spends a third of its time in, not at decoding.
