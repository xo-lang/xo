1's cpu.ghash_blocks) 0.951 seal, 0.957 open (instructions 0.85); the

against Go's 2.27e9 (2.15x; was 3.9x).

tlsrate bulk, quiet Docker linux/arm64, two runs (MB/s median, Go 3475 and
3183 for TLS 1.3, 3779 and 3955 for TLS 1.2): Xo arm64 TLS 1.3 2381 and

read the pairs, not the third digit.

## After the interleaved AES and GHASH kernel (2026-10-22)

`seal_w`, the `seal_into*` paths and `open_w`, `open_from` use
`cpu.aes_gcm_enc/dec` (Xo Lang 1, 0f5353597) for the whole 64 byte chunks.
Mac, `xo bench --ab` (B / A, base is main 3071d4198), N=20000 records of
16 KiB, 10 rounds, load 6:

| workload | wall B/A | 95% | instructions B/A | cpu_ms B/A |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| seal | 0.827 | 0.809 to 0.841 | 1.063 | 0.75 |
| open | 0.790 | 0.777 to 0.797 | 1.059 | 0.75 |

Go row (Go as A, this build as B): seal 1.73x, open 1.83x of Go's wall
(40.2 and 38.9 ms against 69.8 and 71.2). The remaining gap: Go's assembly
works on eight blocks with the round keys held in registers; the kernel
reloads every round key to make room for GHASH's powers, so it runs 6%
more instructions. Next experiments: an 8 block interleave, and the
tlsrate Docker rows (not yet rerun with the fused kernels).
