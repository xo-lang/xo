## Eight blocks a round (Xo Lang 1, cpu.aes_gcm8)

bench/perf/aesgcm, `xo bench --ab` on the Mac, N=60000, 10 rounds, load
high (wall spread 22 to 39%): A = the four block kernel (main), B = eight
blocks a round with the round keys in registers.

| workload | cpu_ms B / A | 95% interval | instructions B / A | wall B / A |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| seal | 0.851 | 0.792 to 0.913 | 0.918 | 0.813 (noise) |
| open | 0.851 | 0.833 to 0.955 | 0.921 | 0.943 (noise) |

Go's twin (A) against B: instructions 2.27e9 against 3.59e9 (seal, 1.58x)
and 2.28e9 against 3.78e9 (open, 1.66x); cpu 1.46x and 1.80x. XO_PROF=cpu

schedule and H's powers, made for every record by internal.crypto_op;
Go's loop makes its cipher once). The kernel runs about 2.5 instructions
a byte, Go's whole loop 2.3: the rest of the gap is per record setup in
this bench, not the bulk loop.


## The loop against Go's loop (seal1, open1)

bench/perf/aesgcm's seal1 and open1 make the key once (internal.crypto_op
`aes.seal*N`, `aes.open*N`), as Go's twin does; the checksums match Go's.
`xo bench --ab` on the Mac, A = Go's twin, B = Xo (eight blocks a round),
N=60000, 10 rounds:

| workload | wall B / A | 95% interval | cpu B / A | instructions B / A |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| seal1 | 1.117 | 1.103 to 1.122 | 1.10 | 1.118 |
| open1 | 1.112 | 1.056 to 1.169 | 1.10 | 1.110 |

Go's crypto/cipher is 1.12x faster on the bulk loop (from 1.73x and 1.83x
with the four block kernel and a key per record). new_gcm computes H^8 to
H^5 on arm64 only (the only reader), four multiplies a key fewer elsewhere.
