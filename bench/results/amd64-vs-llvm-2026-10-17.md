# Xo's amd64 backend against LLVM on linux/amd64 (2026-10-17, Xo Lang 2)

Decision 0254's gate: once amd64 runs the goldens, bench/perf measures it
against LLVM on linux/amd64, ranks where the retargeted arm64 MIR costs
(memory operands, addressing, lea, flags), and records the gap per
workload here.

## Method

- Compiler: wip/fa-amd64 270879e8 with stage 3's crypto lowering
  (wip/xo4b-amd64-crypto bd0f08eb), a linux/amd64 xo cross built here
  with the Go backend (`xo build --backend=go --targets=linux/amd64`),
  and the same commit's linux/arm64 xo for the arm64 rows.
- Every workload built in Docker by that xo, `--backend=amd64` and
  `--backend=llvm` (scripts/docker-linux.sh amd64, under Rosetta on this
  Mac) and `--backend=arm64` and `--backend=llvm` (docker-linux.sh arm64,
  native), then run under `valgrind --tool=cachegrind --cache-sim=no`:
  instructions executed (I refs), deterministic under emulation. The
  checksums printed by both sides agree on every workload.
- Workloads: bench/perf/llvm/xo (the backend suite) and bench/perf/xo
  (collections, list writes, generic code), default sizes.
- "x86 excess" is amd64/LLVM divided by arm64/LLVM: the part of the gap
  that the x86 retarget adds over the shared front half (inlining, loop
  invariants, the runtime's own code), which arm64 has as well.

## Where the retarget loses (from the hot functions' code)

Ranked by how widely they cost; examples from the amd64 assembly
(`xo asm --backend=amd64 --targets=linux/amd64`):

1. Loop rotation is lost. arm64 ends a loop with `cmp; b.lt top`; the x86
   code tests at the top and ends with `jmp` back (memcpy's byte loop:
   rotated on arm64, not on x86). Empty forwarding blocks stay in the
   layout (`.L_b14: jmp .L_b9`), and fib branches `jl b3; jmp b2` around
   a dead `b1: jmp b3`. One or two instructions per iteration of every
   loop: sieve 1.45, iter 1.40, stack 1.50.
2. Two operand ties are not coalesced, and a commutative operation's
   order is not chosen: nbody's inner loop has 26 movapd (a load into
   xmm9, copied to xmm13, then subsd, xmm9 dead after), collatz `movq
   %rsi,%r10; addq $1,%r10; jo; ...; movq %r10,%rsi` a step, fib `movq
   %r12,%rbx; addq %rax,%rbx` then `movq %rbx,%rax`.
3. The multiply's overflow check is arm64's smulh compare translated:
   `movq $3,%rax; imulq %rcx` (rdx:rax), `movq %rdx; sarq $63; cmpq;
   jne`, and a separate `leaq (%rcx,%rcx,2)` for the product: 8
   instructions where `imulq $3,%rcx,%r8; jo` is 2. Most of collatz's
   1.94.
4. Addressing: a constant offset is not folded into the displacement
   (`leaq 8(%r8),%r10; movq (%rsi,%r10)` for `8(%rsi,%r8)`, the same leaq
   twice for a load and a store), and no load is folded into its use
   (`movsd mem; movapd; subsd` for `subsd mem`).
5. Logical immediates: `n & ~31` is `movl $31; movq; movq; notq; andq`
   (arm64's bic of a register) for `andq $-32`.
6. Prologue and epilogue: callee saved registers stored with subq and
   movq and restored with movq and leave; arm64 pairs them (stp, ldp),
   x86's push and pop are fewer instructions. fib and interp.

Shared with arm64 (not x86's): the inlining of map.find and of closures
(get, hof: LLVM inlines them into main), nbody's `bs[i]` address and
bounds check redone in the inner loop, and the runtime's memcpy.

## The runtime's memcpy (fixed here)

branch and copyset copy a map or a list on write each iteration: their
time is std/native/llrt/libcstr.xo memcpy, 99% of branch on both native
backends, 4 times LLVM's vectorized copy of the same loop (an 8 byte
loop tested on `i + 8 <= n`). memcpy, memmove, and memset now move 32
bytes an iteration (two words loaded then stored at a time: the index
form LLVM still vectorizes, at most seven values live so x86-64's
memcpy needs no callee saved register), then words, then (memcpy and
memset from 8 bytes) one overlapping word for the tail; memmove copies
word by word in both directions (a store reaches only bytes already
read). Variants measured on branch (amd64 / LLVM amd64 / arm64 / LLVM
arm64, millions):

## Inlining: closures and calls in loops (2026-10-17)

The shared gaps (arm64 has them too): get kept map.find a call (36
instructions but 14 blocks and 9 call sites, past the inliner's limits),
and hof's fold called its function literal through the closure. The
arm64 and amd64 inliner (selfhost/arm64 inline.xo) now:

- turns a call through a closure made in the same function into a
  direct call of the closure's function when the closure captures
  nothing and the function is one made for value calls (a literal, or a
  named function's `.value` thunk), never a function that catches its
  faults (a task's body); the inliner then takes it;
- inlines a function over the limits only by its size at a call inside
  a loop of its caller, within 60 instructions and 24 blocks, at most 8
  such calls a caller.

map and filter themselves stay calls: their closure calls carry landing
pads (the closure may fault), and the inliner takes no function with
landing pads of its own (decision 0085); inlining those, and
specializing a generic on its closure argument, is the next step for
hof. Amd64 against the run above, and both ratios now:

## Fewer drops, lever (a) by static immortality: no gain, not landed

Skipping the retain and release of a field leaf that resolves statically
(through struct literals and field updates) to an immortal value, a Str
constant, rt.rl_empty, rt.h_zero, or a closure of nothing, with the
verifier exempting those leaves (branch rcimm, not on main): x509.parse
is the same 16,198 instructions, the TLS client +0.04%. Its field
releases are of values that calls return (cbyte.read_element), which no
static rule proves immortal, and the insertion and the verifier can
reach one value through different leaves and disagree (the compiler's
own Builder.cleanup_all failed verification, built without inlining:
gotchas/arm64-inline-verify-fallback.md). An earlier reading of -16%
was that fallback. The levers left: updating a uniquely owned local
struct in place, and moving a multi-value result's fields (b).

## Loop invariant arithmetic (2026-10-17)

compiler/arm64 opt.xo hoist_invariants (after hoist_consts, both native
targets before the x86 retarget): a plain one line add, sub, mul, madd,
msub, shift, logic, sxtw, neg, or mov of registers, whose definition is
its register's only one and whose uses are defined only outside the loop
(an outer loop's counter included), moves to the loop's preheader; hot
blocks only, at most 4 a loop, not in loops that call. On the Mac
(arm64, instructions retired, against main dfb3ee02, the same output):
sortint -6.4%, get -4.1%, push -2.4%, copymut -1.4%, nbody -0.6% (its
bs[i] address is hoisted; its bounds check and the loads of bs[i].x, y,
z, and mass stay: hoisting those needs a store to bs[j].vx proven not to
alias them), the rest within noise (copyset +-2% between runs).

## Fewer drops: immortal old values through the visible literal (2026-10-17)

The compile time route for x509.parse's 588 releases of a field's old
value along a setfield chain (its defaults come from x509.empty, a
52 field struct of b"", "", [], and constants):

- a negative Int literal is a constant in the SSA (compiler/ssa expr.xo),
  not a checked negation with a landing pad; the pads of `max_path: -1`
  and `key_usage: -1` had made x509.empty uninlinable;
- rt.rl_empty and rt.h_zero are leaf intrinsics of the inliner (an
  address each), so x509.empty is a leaf and is inlined;
- reference counting (compiler/ssa rc.xo) emits no retain or release
  for a leaf that is statically immortal, and the verifier (verify.xo)
  never counts one, through one function both use, immortal_leaf: it
  follows struct and tuple literals, field updates, block parameters
  whose every incoming argument is immortal (an inlined call's
  continuation), and calls of a function whose one return is a struct
  literal with that field immortal (immortal_returns: RC runs before the
  inliner, so x509.empty is still a call there). The verifier's landing
  pad edges skip immortal leaves too.

Measured with XO_INLINE_STRICT=1 (gotchas/arm64-inline-verify-fallback.md),
arm64 `xo asm` of bench/perf/tls against main: x509.parse 16,198 to
15,115 instructions (-6.7%) and 2,874 to 2,331 calls (543 releases
gone), the TLS client 417,377 to 414,739 (-0.6%). The compiler's own
strict build verifies.

Binary sizes against main a5022597 (bytes; Go unchanged, the Go backend
has no SSA reference counting): arm64 hello 616,524 to 616,476, the
bench/perf/http JSON server 2,722,299 to 2,689,099 (-1.2%), the TLS client
2,307,978 to 2,291,290 (-0.7%). On LLVM the skip grew the server 1.8% and
the TLS client 2.1% (its own inliner takes more of the cheaper functions)
at no run time gain, so it is off there (ssa Builder.imm_rc, set by
compiler/xo for every backend but llvm): LLVM's TLS client is then
2,355,328 bytes (-208). The TLS handshakes (bench/perf/tls measure.sh,
N=200, x25519, p256, rsa2048, both backends) are unchanged within 0.3%:
certificate parsing is a small part of a handshake. The RC cache key
(rccache.xo, v4) takes the setting and each callee's immortal fields.

## Fewer drops (b): parked (2026-10-17)

x509.parse's releases of a call's result parts (cbyte.read 105,
read_element 60, parse_ai 19, and smaller ones; about 200) are mostly the
updated `var` parameter of a call whose window the caller never reads
again: cbyte.read(tbs, SEQUENCE) returns (Win?, the new tbs), and where
tbs is dead after the call the caller releases the new window's bytes at
once; the callee built and retained a value the caller drops. The rest
are ordinary transfers (an Option released after its payload is taken).
Retain and release pairs of one leaf around these calls are almost
none, so a local "move instead of retain plus release" has little to
work on. The lever is interprocedural: a variant of the callee that does
not produce a dead `var` result, or ownership moved across the call.
Expected gain is small (lever (a) removed 543 calls from x509.parse and
the handshakes did not move). A cleanup noticed on the way: with (a), the
SSA holds more release values after reference counting (x509.parse 1,686
against 1,181 with the skip off) although the final code has 543 fewer
calls; later passes merge them, and the leak checks pass.

## Inlining callees with landing pads at closure sites (decision 0256)

The arm64 and amd64 inliner copies a callee's landing pads (each copied
value's pad the copy of its check, the callee's unwind exit re-raising
into the caller's landing), at calls that pass a closure literal: list.map
and list.filter are inlined into hof_chain and their closure calls become
direct and inlined. On the Mac (arm64, instructions retired, against the
same tree with main's inline.xo): hof -23.7%, maxby -15.8%, every other
workload of bench/perf/xo and of the backend suite within 0.3%, the same
output, binaries the same size (the http JSON server and the TLS client
to the byte, less one). Inlining every function with pads instead made
hof and maxby the same but grew get 4.0%, push 5.4%, iter 8.9% and the
binary 7.9%; operations that keep their operands (list.set, list.take)
stay out (decision 0256 point 3).

## nbody: loop invariant fields and proven indexes (decision 0257)

The inner loop of nbody's advance read bs[i].x, y, z and mass and checked
i against the length twice in every iteration: 53 instructions against
LLVM's 38 (Mac, arm64). compiler/arm64 fieldlicm.xo moves the fields to
the loop's preheader (a set of field vx leaves field x of every element),
and the selector drops a bounds check that a dominating `idx <
list.len` branch proves. nbody: 3,586M to 3,240M instructions retired
(-9.7%; LLVM 2,701M, 1.33 to 1.20 times); 45 instructions an iteration.
The rest of bench/perf/xo and the backend suite within 0.4%, the same
sizes. Left: LLVM's paired loads and stores (ldp/stp, q registers) and
2-wide SIMD for x and y (fsub.2d, faddp), which the arm64 backend does
not emit.

## nbody: bs[i]'s velocity in registers (decision 0257 addendum)

promote_loop keeps fields of element i in registers across a loop whose
other accesses use an index j > i: nbody's bs[i].vx, vy and vz are
loaded before the inner loop and stored once after it. nbody (Mac, arm64,
instructions retired): 3,243M to 3,091M (-4.7%), 1.14 times LLVM
(2,701M); the inner loop is 39 instructions an iteration against LLVM's
38. Against main 1abc879f (before decision 0257) nbody is
-13.8%.

## d register pairs and clustered loads (arm64)

pair_mem joins two d register loads or stores of adjacent words through
one base register into ldp or stp, as it did for x registers, and
cluster_mem (before register allocation) moves such loads and stores of
a block next to each other: a load up past arithmetic, loads and other
words of its base; a store down past arithmetic and other words of its
base; never past a check, a call or a branch. nbody (Mac, arm64): 3,091M
to 2,965M instructions retired (-4.0%; 1.10 times LLVM's 2,701M), all
from energy's loop. advance's inner loop does not pair yet: each proven
list.index still has a landing that splits its block (next: drop those
landings and merge the blocks with merge_straight). The rest of the
corpus is unchanged; the http server and TLS client grow 2 bytes.

## Collections survey (arm64 against LLVM, 2026-10-17)

Per function cachegrind (linux/arm64, main 3ca8e403) and the hot loops'
disassembly, instructions arm64/LLVM: maxby 1.54, update 1.48, grid
1.35, putget 1.28, copymut 1.23 (strings 1.18; its time gap is mostly the
shared map setup). Causes, by how many workloads they hit:

1. Constants rebuilt every iteration in loops that call (update, grid,
   maxby, putget): hoist_consts and hoist_invariants leave a loop with a
   call on its hot path alone, so a 64 bit constant (the hash multiplier,
   a modulus's magic number) costs four instructions each iteration; LLVM
   keeps them in callee saved registers.
