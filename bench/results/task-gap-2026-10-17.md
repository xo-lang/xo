# The single-thread task gap against tokio current_thread (2026-10-17, xo-4)

Workload: bench/perf/conc spawn (200 rounds of 1,000 tasks in a scope,
each adding to an Atomic) at XO_THREADS=1, against bench/perf/conc/rust
with CONCPERF_RT=current (tokio's current-thread runtime).

## Where it stood

| | Xo | tokio | ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| macOS, Mcycles (median of 9) | 65.7 | 57 | 1.15 |
| Linux arm64 (Docker), CPU ms (median of 15) | 26.3 | 19.5 | 1.35 |

Xo ran fewer instructions than tokio (Linux callgrind: 267M against
341M), so the gap was cycles, not work:

- Linux: every runtime atomic was a call of an outline helper written as
  a load and store exclusive loop (selfhost/macho's elflink, decision
  0182), about 15 a spawn; tokio's are single LSE instructions (about
  21 a spawn). Cachegrind: Xo's L1 data misses 3.66M against 2.12M.
- macOS: `sample` put dyld's `_tlv_get_addr` at about 9% of the main
  thread: 18 thread-local accesses a spawn (tokio's 5), each a TLV call
  across a call.

## What changed

- 60c69267: run_claimed reads the thread's table once before the task
  runs and once after (7 thread-local accesses to 2).
- 00efa325: scope_add hands its worker record to defer_check when it is
  the thread's own (scope_add_at; a first version also did so for the
  root thread's spawns into the root worker's ring, and multi-threaded
  spawn took 1.7 times the cycles: the gate's conc run caught it), and
  defer_check's one-worker root test comes before its count and clock.
- 2be14c89: alloc takes a magazine for an empty class itself (refill's
  take written out: decision 0207 lets alloc call no function), so
  halloc no longer calls refill before every allocation (spawn 281.1 to
  275.9 M instructions).
- 8a506f33 (Xo lang 5): elflink's outline atomics use LSE when AT_HWCAP
  has ATOMICS.

## Where it stands

| | Xo | tokio | ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| macOS, Mcycles (median of 9, load 15 to 17) | 59.7 to 60.0 | 56.2 to 56.7 | about 1.06 |
| Linux arm64 (Docker), CPU ms (median of 15, 2 rounds) | 18.9, 19.0 | 19.6, 19.7 | 0.96, 0.97 |

The multi-threaded conc workloads (spawn, fanout, pingpong, mutex,
rwmutex, pmap) were within noise of main's at both XO_THREADS settings
in the gates of 60c69267 and 00efa325.

## Tried and dropped

- Publishing a task's end without its lock and waiters' wake when its
  handle is gone (the count -2, task_wanted's test): at the end of the
  run the count is not -2 in this workload, so the test only adds a load
  (272.6 to 273.6 M instructions).
- A count of a scope's background tasks so scope_close skips its
  cancellation walk when there are none: +37 instructions a spawn
  (272.7 to 280.2 M) for a load and a branch in scope_add, likely its
  inlining; the walk is cheap next to that.

## The next experiment (parked)

The task layout: the hot fields (state, run, fl and fenv, scope, result,
the flags, the scope list's link) in the first 64 to 128 bytes, the
rarely used ones (the 56 byte cancellation node, the three fault
strings, the awaiters' mutex and wait list, the budget) after them or in
a side allocation made when needed, and only the hot lines zeroed at
spawn. Expected: a few percent on macOS (what is left is mostly cold
lines: Linux cachegrind's misses per task were in scope_join 1.4,
halloc 1.2, run_claimed 0.77). The lead's three checks: every reader of
the layout (tasks.xo, and the copies of its offsets in pool.xo, sched.xo
and dump.xo: T_STATE, T_SNEXT, T_CTX, T_LOC_*), with a test that fails
on a stale offset; the multi-threaded sched sweep and HTTP p99 (false
sharing between a worker and a waker); and a build from the 0.19.0 seed
reaching the fixed point (the compiler does not read the layout, so
expected to pass).
