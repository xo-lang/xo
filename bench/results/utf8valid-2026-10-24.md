# A 2-byte case in scan.seq_len: 5.2% off non-ASCII JSON decoding, 2026-10-24

`scan.utf8_valid` sits under every Bytes-to-Str conversion that has to
check its input, and its per-rune work is `scan.seq_len`: a ladder of
lead-byte ranges, then a loop over the continuation bytes, then the
size. On text that is mostly 2-byte runes (Greek, Cyrillic, Latin with
accents) that loop and its setup are most of the cost, so `seq_len` now
answers the 2-byte case first and returns, and the ladder handles only
the 3- and 4-byte leads and the invalid ones.

## The change

std/native/llrt/scan.xo, `seq_len`: if the lead byte is in `0xC2` to
`0xDF` (the whole valid 2-byte range, which excludes the overlong `C0`
and `C1`), one continuation byte in `0x80` to `0xBF` gives size 2, and
anything else gives size 1. The ladder's own `C2` to `DF` arm is gone.

## A/B, one revision, A = the change's xo and std, B = origin/main's

`xo bench --ab`, 7 rounds, A and B the same program source, each built
by its own xo. B/A is what B runs over A, so above 1.0 means the change
runs fewer instructions; the change's own percentage is given too.
Go's encoding/json and regexp are the third column, for reference.

| workload | A (change) | B (base) | B/A | change | Go |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| JSON decode, Greek and Cyrillic | 6,277,602,247 | 6,623,109,468 | 1.055 | -5.22% | 18,206,803,621 |
| JSON decode, ASCII only | 3,976,368,116 | 3,969,994,909 | 0.998 | +0.16% | 14,485,384,321 |
| JSON decode, CJK and emoji | 5,647,540,390 | 5,641,085,183 | 0.999 | +0.11% | 15,028,708,628 |
| `[\p{L}]+` find_all over mixed text | 11,926,722,367 | 11,926,598,862 | 1.000 | 0.0% | 12,532,602,860 |

The ASCII row is the control: a pure ASCII document never calls
`seq_len` (its runs are skipped 16 bytes at a time by `span`), so its
0.16% is code layout, not the change. The CJK row pays one extra range
test per rune, 0.11% over 5.6 G. The JSON row against Go is 2.9 times
fewer instructions after the change, 2.75 before.

## Coverage

std/native/llrt/scan_test.xo gained the 2-byte bounds (`C2 82`, `DF
BF`), the overlong leads (`C0 80`, `C1 BF`), a bad continuation (`C3
41`), a lone continuation (`80`, `BF`), the surrogate range at both ends
(`ED 9F BF` valid, `ED BF BF` not), and truncation at the end of the
range for 2-, 3- and 4-byte sequences, plus a `utf8_valid` test that
rejects each malformed sequence inside a run of ASCII.
