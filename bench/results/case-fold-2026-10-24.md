# Case folding eight bytes at a time, and a push_word for a replacement: 2026-10-24

Follow-up on `rune-classes-2026-10-23.md` (to_lower and to_upper at 0.63 to
0.66 of Go's instructions on mixed text, 0.33 on ASCII) and on
`bytestransform-2026-10-24.md`, where DS-2 measured the replace path's
remainder as calls (`cabi.obj_rs_append_bytes` 22.7% flat, 58.9%
cumulative).

`XO_PROF=cpu` on a transformation program (`lower_u`, `upper_u` and
`replace` over an ASCII text and a mixed text repeated 4000 and 2000
times, 100 Hz, 128 samples, 1280 ms):

| flat | cum | function |
|---|---|---|
| 15.6% | 15.6% | `raw.decode` (the UTF-8 decode of every non-ASCII rune) |
| 14.8% | 65.6% | `unicode.case_append` (its own loop) |
| 10.2% | 10.2% | `obj.addw` |
| 9.4% | 19.5% | `obj.fold` (the per-byte ASCII case change of every appended run) |
| 9.4% | 46.1% | `cabi.obj_rs_append_bytes` |
| 6.3% | 10.9% | `obj.move_at` and `libcstr.move_small` (the copies) |
| 6.3% | 7.8% | `scan.find_bytes` (replace's search) |
| 6.3% | 9.4% | `raw.push_rune` (a changed non-ASCII rune) |

So the appended runs were copied and then walked again a byte at a time,
and replace paid a general append for a replacement that usually fits a
word.

## What changed (std/native/llrt only)

- `obj.fold`: eight bytes at a time. The letters' bytes are found with a
  range test on seven bit copies of the word (`between8`), a byte at or
  above 0x80 never counting, and 0x20 is added to each letter's byte
  (subtracted when raising), which cannot carry into the next byte. The
  last n % 8 bytes keep the byte loop.
- `strings.replace_build`: a replacement of 1 to 8 bytes is held as a word
  once (`word_of`, built with `wrapping_shl`) and stored with
  `rt.rs_push_word` at every match, instead of `rt.rs_append_bytes`'s
  reserve, copy and mode check; an empty replacement appends nothing.

## What did not work

Skipping the fold pass for a run with no letter in the target case (a
`span_case` range test before the append): B / A instructions 1.013 and
wall within noise on the 12 round A/B above. The runs it skips are the
short ASCII runs between non-ASCII runes, a few bytes each, so the test
cost more than the fold it saved. Reverted.
