# Str.to_lower and to_upper: a std-side word unroll cannot close the ASCII
# gap to LLVM, 2026-10-25

The ASCII case rows of `bench/perf/unicode` (`lower`, `upper`) run at 1.755
and 1.697 of LLVM's instructions on native arm64, where the mixed rows
(`lower_u`, `upper_u`, `eqlower`) are already 0.82 to 0.85 of LLVM's. The
gap is the ASCII fold loop: clang widens it to 16 byte NEON, and the arm64
backend has no vector instructions, so it runs the same source as eight
byte SWAR. An unroll of that loop in std buys arm64 14 percent of the row
and costs LLVM 1.7 percent, so it is not kept.

## The measurement

`bench/perf/unicode`, one xo built at `origin/main` (b7faeedb3) building
the program for each backend, best of 3 runs, `/usr/bin/time -l`:

| workload | arm64 instructions | llvm instructions | arm64 / llvm |
|---|---|---|---|
| lower (ASCII text) | 293,123,772 | 167,017,351 | 1.755 |
| upper (ASCII text) | 293,182,169 | 172,707,019 | 1.697 |
| lower_u (mixed text) | 3,045,109,698 | 3,694,551,464 | 0.824 |
| upper_u (mixed text) | 3,041,612,973 | 3,585,429,886 | 0.848 |
| eqlower (case compare) | 8,047,232,398 | 9,546,621,249 | 0.843 |

## Where the ASCII row goes

`XO_PROF=cpu XO_PROF_HZ=1000` on the arm64 build of a copy of the benchmark
with `REPS` 4000 (296 ms of wall, 51 samples, so the split is coarse):

| frame | flat |
|---|---|
| `obj.fold_copy_lo` (the SWAR fold) | 43.1% |
| `sample` (the workload's own checksum, `i += 61` over the result) | 41.2% |
| `scan.span` (the ASCII run scan, `rt.bytes_high16` 16 bytes a step) | 11.8% |
| `obj.reserve` | 2.0% |

The fold is therefore about 45 percent of the row's instructions, and it
is already the SWAR the lead's plan describes: `rs_append_bytes` hands an
ASCII run to `fold_copy_lo` or `fold_copy_up`, which load eight bytes,
test the letters with `between8` (two broadcasts adds and two masks),
shift each letter's high bit down by two and add or subtract it, so 0x20
lands in each letter's byte, and store the word back. The per rune path
is not involved: an ASCII byte never reaches it.

The arm64 loop, from `otool -tv` of `_xo_f_obj_2ers__append__bytes`, is
15 instructions for eight bytes: `sub` and `cmp #8` and `b.lt` for the
step, `ldr`, `and` with 0x7f7f7f7f7f7f7f7f, two `add`s, `bic`, `and` with
0x8080808080808080, `bic`, `lsr #2`, `add`, `str`, and the `add` and back
edge.

## What was tried

### Form 1, two loops: 16 bytes a step, then the eight byte loop

The 16 byte step costs 21 instructions (one `add` for the address, `ldp`
for both loads, 6 a word for the mask, 2 a word for the shift and the add,
`stp` for both stores), 10.5 a word against the base loop's 15.

`xo bench --ab`, 10 rounds, both programs built from the same `main.xo`,
A = main's std and B = the change, each built by its own xo, machine load
12 or higher through the run so the wall rows are not read (the load
column says so); instructions, B / A:

| workload | backend | A | B | B / A |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| lower | arm64 | 293,142,905 | 252,541,886 | 0.861 |
| upper | arm64 | 293,131,096 | 252,528,398 | 0.861 |
| lower_u | arm64 | 3,044,787,576 | 3,065,542,180 | 1.007 |
| upper_u | arm64 | 3,041,994,868 | 3,056,520,854 | 1.005 |
| eqlower | arm64 | 8,047,430,095 | 8,107,480,611 | 1.007 |
| lower | llvm | 167,026,674 | 169,914,509 | 1.017 |
| upper | llvm | 172,737,607 | 175,649,112 | 1.017 |
| lower_u | llvm | 3,694,464,048 | 3,704,587,505 | 1.003 |
| upper_u | llvm | 3,585,544,509 | 3,585,752,814 | 1.000 |
| eqlower | llvm | 9,547,532,306 | 9,591,635,645 | 1.005 |

Checksums matched on both sides of every row.

Two things are wrong with that shape for a std change:

- the mixed rows pay about 5 instructions a call for a second setup of
  the two broadcast constants, which the arm64 backend materializes again
  per loop rather than keeping in registers; the mixed rows call
  `rs_append_bytes` about 20,000 times a build of the text, which is the
  0.5 to 0.7 percent there;
- LLVM carries two loops to widen instead of one: both builds vectorize
  the fold (NEON `and.16b`, `add.16b`, `bic.16b`; 37 `and.16b` in the
  base's `rs_append_bytes`, 55 in the change's), and the two loop form
  retires 2.9 M more instructions on the row.

### Form 2, one loop with a second word under a test

Setting the two constants up once and doing the second word only when 16
bytes are left sounds like it removes both costs. Its arm64 loop is 29
instructions for 16 bytes against 30 for the base loop, because the second
step needs its own test and branch and the back edge is still there, so
the row could only reach about 0.97 against form 1's 0.861. It was
dropped from the disassembly, before a wall measurement.

## What is left

The fold's eight to ten instructions a word are at the SWAR floor: the
mask needs two broadcast adds and two masks, and the change to a letter's
byte is a shift and an add, and no arrangement of them that was written
here or in the earlier fold work (`bench/results/case-fold-2026-10-24.md`,
`casefold-copy-2026-10-24.md`) removes an instruction.

The remaining factor 3.3 on the fold loop (1.31 instructions a byte
against LLVM's 0.4) is vector width. Decision 0366 measured the same
conclusion from the other side: a hand written two lane fold loop in the
backend was 0.419 of native's instructions and 1.71 times its speed on the
loop, short of its 2 times bar. Reaching LLVM's cost needs a vector fold
the arm64 backend emits, in the shape `rt.bytes_high16` already has
(`compiler/arm64/calls.xo` lowers it to one `vld1` and a `cmhi`/`shrn`
pair): a new intrinsic that folds 16 bytes, lowered to NEON on arm64, SSE2
on amd64, a scalar loop for wasm, and the SWAR loop for LLVM. That is a
compiler change that touches the fixed point, and it needs its own
measurement against this table, not against a guess.

## The boundary rows

`scripts/stdcorpus/bytes.go` (`caseBoundaryCases`, in the `bytes` corpus'
`CASE_CASES`, whose expectations are Go's `bytes.ToLower` and
`bytes.ToUpper`) now covers every length below, at and just past a word
(0, 7, 8, 9, 15, 16 and 17), a byte at or above 0x80 at each position of
an eight byte and a sixteen byte word, and 0x40, 0x5b, 0x60 and 0x7b next
to the A-Z and a-z ranges. They pass on main's std on arm64 and LLVM, and
they are the rows any future fold change has to keep.
