# Bytes and Str find: Go's second byte prefix test, and the short-haystack brute force that failed, 2026-10-24

Two attempts at closing the search part of the `cut` row's gap against Go
(`bench/results/bytessearch-2026-10-24.md`: cut at 1.48 of Go's total
instructions, 1.74 net of the loop, with `scan.find_bytes` at 22 to 26% of
the profile flat and up to 54% cumulative). Both were measured with
`xo bench --ab --rounds=10` (decision 0340) on two binaries built from the
same revision, differing only in `std/native/llrt/scan.xo` (`XO_STD` pointed
at a copy of the checkout's std holding the committed file), with
`XO_INLINE_STRICT=1`. Ratio is B/A, so above 1 means B (the base) uses more
instructions and the change is a win. Logs:
`~/xo-lead/agents/pi2-short2-cmd.log` (attempt 1),
`~/xo-lead/agents/pi2-short3-cmd.log` (attempt 2); JSON per workload in
`/tmp/pi2-short/ab-*.json` and `/tmp/pi2-short3/ab-*.json`.

## Attempt 1, rejected: Go's short-haystack brute force

Go's `bytes.Index` takes a brute-force path for `len(s) <= bytealg.MaxBruteForce`
(64 on amd64; arm64's `IndexString` compares in registers). Ported as
`index_short`: for a haystack of at most 64 bytes, walk the candidate starts
byte by byte, compare `sub[0]` and `sub[1]`, and call `eq_at` for the rest
only on a match. It agreed with the base on every corpus case and every
benchmark output, and it was slower everywhere it mattered:

| workload | change, M | base, M | base / change |
|---|---|---|---|
| cut | 87.81 | 73.27 | 0.83 (change 19.9% worse) |
| split_after | 234.75 | 220.78 | 0.94 (6.3% worse) |
| strsplit split | 182.15 | 171.96 | 0.94 (5.9% worse) |
| strsplit split_multi | 148.73 | 132.29 | 0.89 (12.4% worse) |
| strsplit split_absent | 123.00 | 86.27 | 0.70 (42.6% worse) |
| all other rows | | | within noise |

The cause is the skip, not the compare: a byte-at-a-time candidate walk
cannot beat `find_byte`'s 16-byte word scan (`eq16_1`), which is what Go gets
from `IndexByte` in that same path. Short haystacks are not
cheap-to-scan-byte haystacks. Nothing was kept from this attempt except its
usefulness as a bound: the search's skip is the part that works.

## Attempt 2, landed: Go's second byte test before the whole compare

`bytes.Index` tests `s[i+1] == c1` before `Equal(s[i:i+n], sep)`. Our loop
called `eq_at(b, i + 1, sub, 1, m - 1)` on every candidate whose first byte
matched. The change keeps the word-scan skip and adds Go's prefix test, so a
candidate that cannot match is rejected by one load and one compare, and
`m == 2` never calls `eq_at`. It also adds Go's `n == len(s)` case (one
Equal, no scan) ahead of the loop.

| workload | change, M | base, M | base / change | interval |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| cut | 71.85 | 75.18 | 1.046 | 1.016 to 1.054 |
| split_after | 219.81 | 221.59 | 1.008 | 1.003 to 1.015 |
| strsplit split_multi | 127.52 | 133.32 | 1.046 | 1.042 to 1.049 |
| every other row | | | within noise | |

So: 4.4% fewer instructions on `cut`, 4.3% on `split_multi`, 0.8% on
`split_after`, and no regression anywhere in the 17 rows (the nine
bytessearch workloads and the eight strsplit ones). The rows that moved are
exactly the ones with a multi-byte separator found often, which is the
candidate set the prefix test filters: `cut` uses `", "`, `" "`, `"abc"`,
`""`, `"✓"`, `":"` over short texts, and `split_multi` splits on a
multi-byte separator.

This is a modest win. It does not close the row: the remaining `cut` gap is
the caller's loop and the view lifecycle, which is the ownership item filed
with Lang 1 (a view returned from borrows and consumed before any store
keeps its retain and release; repro in `/tmp/pi2-viewrc`), plus
`prelude.slice_check` in the successful path (Lang 1's tw375 makes it
inlinable).

## Corpus

`scripts/stdcorpus/strsearch.go` gained `shortHayCases`, 22 cases run
through the existing INDEX_CASES table (Go's `strings.Index`, `LastIndex`
and `Contains` as the oracle): a one byte and a two byte separator, a
separator as long as the haystack, a match at the first and at the last
start, a near miss on the second byte (including at the last start),
non-ASCII separators and haystacks, and haystacks of exactly 64 and 65 bytes
with the match at the last start and absent, plus 63 bytes ending `ab` and
the same at 64 and 66 bytes with a two byte rune. They pin both attempts'
edge conditions and the boundary between the paths regardless of which
search is in the file.
