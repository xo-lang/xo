# Steadiness: Xo against Go 1.26.1 (2026-10-30, Xo lang 5)

The owner's question (2026-10-30): does Xo's reference counting, with no
collector and so no GC pauses, give a steadier far tail than Go? The twin
servers of bench/perf/jitter (`GET /hello`, 6 bytes; `GET /alloc`, a 200
item page built and encoded as JSON per request, 14,683 bytes on both)
under its load generator (raw HTTP/1.1, the client's GC off, every
latency kept).

- Host: the linux/arm64 offload host (12 CPUs, a container), the job
  alone on it. Server on CPUs 0-5 (taskset), load generator on 6-11.
- 20 rounds, the two servers alternating first; 10 s measured after 2 s
  of warmup per run. CPU steal ticks 0 in every run; the load average
  about 9 (the run's own server and client).
- xo built at the bench's commit (main 68ab0dc7b plus the bench; the open loop at c8ecc7d0c), Xo's
  native backend; Go's default GOGC.
- No real time priority: the container lacks CAP_SYS_NICE (chrt -f
  fails), so a run on SCHED_FIFO waits for the owner to recreate it.

Paired by round, Xo's p99.9 is below Go's in 20 of 20 rounds for both
/alloc rows and /hello at 256 connections, and 19 of 20 at /hello 32; its
max is below Go's in 20 of 20 for /alloc, but only 8 and 4 of 20 for

Xo's req/s CV of 0.068 at /hello 32 is round 1 alone (255k against 333k
to 367k in the other 19): the first run of the job, right after the
build.

Reading:

- /hello: Xo serves 27% (32 connections) and 83% (256) more requests a
  second at 42% less CPU a request, with p99 2.9x and 3.2x lower.
- /alloc: Go is CPU bound (130 to 170 us a request on 6 CPUs caps it near
  40k a second), so a closed loop queues behind it and its tail is mostly
  queueing, not collection pauses: p99 7.4 ms and 64 ms. Xo builds and
  encodes the same page in 33 us of CPU, 4 to 5x less (Go's fmt.Sprintf and
  reflection based encoding/json against Xo's interpolation and derived
  encoder; the strconv section below measures the fmt.Sprintf part), so
  its saturation point is 4 to 5x higher (a closed loop saturates both:
  184k a second at 32.5 us is the same 6 CPUs). A closed loop shows which
  server is faster, not which is steadier at the same load: that is the
  open loop below.

## Open loop (the same offered load on both: each of 64 connections on a fixed schedule)

`loadgen -rate R -c 64`: R requests a second in all, latency counted from
each request's scheduled send time, so a stall shows in every request
queued behind it (no coordinated omission). /alloc at 10k and 20k a
second (below Go's closed loop capacity of about 23k at 32 connections),
/hello at 100k and 200k. 20 rounds, CPU steal 0 in every run, load
average about 5. Both servers met every rate (req/s CV 0.000).

## Correction: the generator's send lateness (2026-11-01, Xo lang 4)

The open loop's latency counts from each request's scheduled send time,
so it includes how late the generator itself sent. The generator is a Go
program whose goroutines sleep until their send time, and the p50 of
about 700 us turned out to be mostly that lateness, not the server's:
at 2,500 and 5,000 a second Go shows it too. `loadgen` now also reports
`late_*` (the send against its schedule) and `svc_*` (the send to the
response's end). /alloc, 5 rounds, xo at the `loadgen` change, the same
host and CPU split, medians in us:

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2,500 | Xo | 705 | 1,612 | 588 | 1,446 | 106 | 408 | 623 | 74.8 |
| 2,500 | Xo XO_NETPOLL=0 | 715 | 1,592 | 597 | 1,443 | 113 | 342 | 541 | 66.0 |
| 2,500 | Go | 726 | 1,674 | 545 | 1,437 | 148 | 552 | 755 | 155.6 |

- The finding below that Xo's median at light load is worse than Go's
  (/alloc at 10,000: 680 against 199 us) does not hold as a server
  latency: Xo's service p50 there is 138 us against Go's 130, and its
  service p99 262 us against Go's 1,080. Most of the 680 was the generator
  sending late. The cause it guessed, waking parked workers, is not
  supported by this.
- What the generator's lateness depends on is not settled: it is on its
  own CPUs, yet at 10,000 it differs by server (late p50 479 us with Xo,
  138 with XO_NETPOLL=0, 57 with Go). A guess, not measured: a slower
  server keeps responses arriving, which keeps the generator's runtime
  awake and its timers on time.
- Below about 15,000 a second the totals and any comparison of them are
  mostly the generator's; read the service columns. The open loop table
  above and the tail figures in the answer below are totals.
- Service latency still has a limit: it starts at the send, so it does not
  count queueing a late send hid, and it includes the generator's own wake
  on the response.
- Settled 2026-11-02 (jitter-low-rate-p50-2026-11-02.md): the lateness is
  Go's runtime sleeping in a millisecond epoll wait when every P of the
  generator is idle; `loadgen -awake`, now the open loop's default, keeps
  a goroutine runnable, and Xo's /alloc p50 is then 91 to 106 us from
  5,000 to 20,000 a second. In this section's table the total, late and
  service columns all carry it (service through the late wake on the
  response); the CPU column reflects the bunched arrivals the late
  generator made (see above).
- A difference that remains in the service columns: at 15,000 worker
  polling's p99 is 301 us against the poller thread's 131, and at 20,000
  its p99.9 is 294 against 160, though its p50 is lower at both. Five
  rounds, not paired by round here.

## Answer to the question

- Under allocation (/alloc) at the same load, Xo's far tail is steadier:
  at 20k a second Go's p99 is 10.4 ms, its p99.9 17 ms and its worst
  request 47 ms, against Xo's 1.0, 1.6 and 8.1 ms; at 10k Xo's p99.9 is
  still lower (1.8 against 2.6 ms) in 19 of 20 rounds. Go at 20k runs at
  189 us of CPU a request, about 63% of its 6 CPUs, where collection and
  its assists add to every queue; Xo spends 47 us.
- With almost no allocation (/hello), the two tails are close: p99.9
  about 1 ms on both (Go's lower in 14 of 20 rounds at 100k), Xo's p99
  3x and 4x lower; both reach 10 ms worst cases (a host pause, seen in
  the closed loop too).
- Corrected above (2026-11-01): the next point's median is mostly the
  generator's lateness, not the server's. As first written:
- Not steadier: Xo's median at light load. /alloc at 10k gives Xo a p50
  of 680 us against Go's 199 (and Xo's own 93 us at 20k), and 45.8 us of
  CPU a request against 33 at saturation. Not measured further here (the
  session paused); the likely cause is waking parked workers (a fiber
  readied by a poll while every worker sleeps, decision 0324's poller
  thread ticks of 0.5 to 10 ms), to check with XO_RTSTATS wake counts and
  XO_NETPOLL=0 at the same rate.
- So the hypothesis holds for the far tail under allocation, where GC
  pauses and assists are the difference. The first version of this page
  added that it does not hold for the median at light load, where Xo's
  idle workers cost it latency; the correction above withdraws that.

## Go /alloc with strconv (2026-11-01, Xo Lang 4)

`GET /alloc-strconv` is `/alloc` with strconv.Itoa and concatenation in
place of fmt.Sprintf, still encoding/json: the same 14,683 byte body. Go
1.26.1, the same host and CPU split, 32 connections, 10 rounds, the two
handlers alternating, 10 s per run after 2 s of warmup, no errors, CPU steal
0 in every run (raw lines: ~/xo-lead/notes/xo-lang-4/out/strconv.txt). Medians, CPU with its range over the rounds:

| load | handler | req/s | CPU us/req | p50 us | p99 us | service p50 us | service p99 us |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| closed loop | /alloc | 23,022 | 170.8 (166.9 to 173.7) | 712 | 7,322 | | |
| closed loop | /alloc-strconv | 25,109 | 144.6 (141.0 to 145.5) | 562 | 6,322 | | |
| 10k a second | /alloc | 10,000 | 144.6 (142.7 to 145.8) | 187 | 1,797 | 126 | 961 |
| 10k a second | /alloc-strconv | 10,000 | 129.1 (128.0 to 130.2) | 193 | 2,080 | 124 | 1,033 |

- The formatting idiom is 11% to 15% of Go's CPU a request, and the ranges
  do not overlap. Most of the gap to Xo's 33 us stays: about 4.4x in the
  closed loop against 5.2x (Xo's figure is from the table above, an
  earlier run, not interleaved with these).
- What is left is not separated here: encoding/json's reflection, net/http,
  and the collector. A next step that splits them: a Go /alloc with a hand
  written encoder writing the same bytes.
- Saving that CPU did not move latency at 10k a second: service p50 is
  level, and p99 is slightly worse for strconv (quartiles not compared, so
  no claim either way).
