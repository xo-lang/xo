# TLS performance: the Xo TLS against Mbed TLS (2026-10-15)

The lead's TLS performance item (decision 0202; the zero-C push's 5%
bar). bench/perf/tls: crypto/tls servers (serve/, one listener per
configuration, HTTP/1.1 only so every request is a new connection and a
full handshake, 16 KiB records) and std/http's client (main.xo) built
with the Xo TLS (tls13.xo, tls12.xo; the default) and with Mbed TLS
(`XO_LLRT_C=tlsio`). Measured on the development Mac (Apple M-series,
arm64), `bench/perf/tls/measure.sh bin/xo OUT arm64 llvm` with N=50
handshakes and a 16 MiB body, through heavy.sh; the client's
instructions retired (min of 5 runs, /usr/bin/time -l) less its start
alone (N=0: 28.0M arm64, 25.2M LLVM, the same for both TLS sides), per
handshake or per MiB (a bulk run less one handshake). Wall times have
the 10 ms resolution of /usr/bin/time, so the Mbed bulk rates are rough.
The server side (tlssrv.xo against Mbed) follows once it lands.

## Step 2: P-256 field arithmetic and a fixed base table (nistec.xo)

The Montgomery product, sum, and difference for 4 limbs (P-256's field
and scalars) unrolled over local words (the generic CIOS loops over
limbs in memory stay for P-384); then r = [k]G on P-256 from a table of
j 16^w G (std/native/llrt/nistec_g256.xo, 64 windows of 15 affine
points in the Montgomery domain, made by scripts/nistecg256): 64
complete additions, each window's entry chosen under masks from all 15,
in place of 256 doublings and 64 additions. Keygen, ECDSA signing, and
the u1 G of verification use it. Byte identical on tls_nistec,
tls_verify, tls_x509, tls_rsa, crypto_prims, and the tls goldens.

## Step 3: P-384's field unrolled, RSA's modulus setup (nistec.xo, bigmod.xo)

The 6 limb Montgomery product, sum, and difference (P-384's field and
scalars) unrolled as step 2's; and bigmod.modulus's R mod m and R^2 mod
m (every RSA operation sets its modulus up): R mod m from 2^(bits-1),
doubled up to 2^(64 n) (one doubling for RSA-2048, where 2 x 64 n = 4,096
doublings of the whole number were), and R^2 mod m by a square and
double chain over the bits of 64 n (12 Montgomery squarings), as Go's
rr; the modulus is public. Byte identical on tls_rsa, tls_nistec, and
the tls goldens.

## Step 4: a doubling formula and inversion chains (nistec.xo)

Go's dedicated doubling (Renes, Costello and Batina's algorithm 6 for
a = -3: 13 products and fewer sums than the complete addition) for
pt_mul's doublings; inversion in P-256's field by Go's addition chain
(255 squarings and 12 products where square and multiply took 256 and
128), the other exponents (P-256's n - 2, P-384's p - 2 and n - 2) by
a fixed 4 bit window over the public exponent (saving 91 to 208
products each). Constant time as before (the steps follow the public
exponent only). Byte identical on tls_nistec, tls_verify, tls_x509,
tls_rsa, and the tls goldens.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| ECDSA P-256, X25519 | 15.9 | 14.7 | 5.3 | 2.8x |
| ECDSA P-256, P-256 | 20.2 | 18.6 | 12.9 | 1.4x |

RSA: rsa.xo's private operation already works by CRT with Montgomery
exponentiation (a 4 bit window, no allocation in the loop) and Go's
check of the result. The RSA-2048 client handshake's samples are the CA's
ECDSA P-256 check and X25519 (the bench's CA is P-256); bigmod.mul_at is
about 3% of them, so what is left there is the field products (the add
with carry intrinsic of the proposal below).

## Where the time goes (sample, LLVM)

- Handshakes: `nistec.mul` (the generic Montgomery product, CIOS over
  limbs in memory), `nistec.add` and `nistec.sub` take about 90% of the
  X25519 handshake's samples; the X25519 exchange itself
  (`ed25519.fe_mul`, `fe_sq`) about 5%. With RSA-2048, `bigmod.add_at`
  is the top frame, then nistec (the CA's signature).
- ChaCha20-Poly1305: `chacha.qr` about 70% (the quarter round on a
  List[Int] passed as var: a bounds check and a uniqueness check on
  every word), Poly1305's `tag` about 17%.
- AES-GCM: `aesgcm.shift_rows`, `sbox`, `add_round_key` (bitsliced AES,
  four blocks a pass, about 25 instructions a word in shift_rows) and
  `Gcm.mul` (constant time carry-less multiply). The code is already
  tight; Mbed uses the CPU's AES and PMULL instructions (about 1 cycle a
  byte). Mbed's 14M instructions a MiB is the rest of the stack (reads,
  copies, HTTP), a floor for both sides.

## Step 7: the arm64 code of the field products (2026-10-15)

From xo-2b's sweep (bench/results/zero-c-perf-sweep-2026-10-15.md):
P-256 was 1.54x Mbed on arm64 and 1.04x on LLVM from the same source,
nistec.mul4 34 to 48% of the handshakes' samples. Three changes:

- the arm64 emitter selects an add or subtract and its carry or borrow
  (cpu.carry, cpu.borrow, a few values later in the block) as one adds
  or subs and a cset (selfhost/arm64 calls.xo add_with_flag), where it
  made add, cmn, cset;
- mul4 and mul6 lose the terms of 0 my generator left (adds of 0,
  carries of 0) and sum each pair of carries with a wrapping add, not a
  checked `+` (adds, b.vs, and a fault call each);
- the SSA builder folds `+` and `-` of two Int constants that do not
  overflow (`MLIMBS + 8` was a mov and an add at every load).

mul4 on arm64: 722 instructions to 307 (mul6 is 728, with spills).
Measured against the same tree without them (zero-c 694cc542, N=200,
the client's minimum instructions retired over 5 runs, M):

## Step 11: P-256's field by shifts, no dispatch (2026-10-16)

A profile of one handshake (macOS `sample`, N=4000, LLVM) put P-256 at
about 70% of the X25519 handshake's compute and most of the RSA-2048
one's (the bench's CA is P-256, and the client's RSA work is a public
key check): the client verifies two P-256 signatures per handshake.
Two changes in nistec.xo, constant time as before:

- mul4p: the Montgomery product for P-256's field prime, its reduction
  by shifts (p's limbs are 2^64 - 1, 2^32 - 1, 0, and 2^64 - 2^32 + 1,
  and -1/p mod 2^64 is 1: t0 + q m0 is q 2^64 for q = t0, q m1 + q is
  q 2^32, m2 is 0, q m3 is q 2^64 - q 2^32 + q), generated by
  scripts/nistecmul4p.py; mul uses it for P-256's field only (P-224
  has 4 limbs too, and the new TestNistecDiff checks every NIST curve
  against crypto/ecdsa);
- pt_add256 and pt_double256: the same formulas on mul4p and on add
  and subtract with the prime's limbs as constants, without the
  per operation dispatch on the curve record.

M instructions a handshake (N=200, the client's minimum over 3 runs
less the process alone), main 43254a4c against the change:

| Handshake | Backend | Before | mul4p | + no dispatch | Mbed | Now/Mbed |

## Step 12: add with carry in (decision 0231's addendum, 2026-10-16)

cpu.adc and cpu.adc_carry (x + y + cin and its carry) as one
instruction: adcs chains on arm64 (a chain of pairs, each carry the
next pair's carry in, is one adds or cmp and adcs, then adcs, and one
cset), llvm.uadd.with.overflow chains on LLVM, which become adcs.
mul4p (scripts/nistecmul4p.py) now sums with them: per word of g a
chain of the products' low halves, a chain of the high halves, and one
chain for q p / 2^64 (its limbs shifts of q). LLVM's mul4p is 153
instructions with 32 adcs, where it was 232 with 38 cmn and 24 cset.

## Steps 13 and 14: P-256 squaring, X25519 on 64 bit limbs (2026-10-16)

- **sqr4p** (scripts/nistecmul4p.py): the P-256 field square from the
  6 cross products once, doubled, plus the 4 diagonal squares (10
  products where mul4p makes 16), then 4 Montgomery reduction rounds over
  the 8 word square, each one adc chain with mul4p's addend of shifts.
  mul takes it when both operands are the same P-256 field element
  (inversion's 255 squarings), and pt_double256 for its 3 squares.
- **X25519's field** (scripts/x25519fe64.py, x25519.xo): four 64 bit
  limbs kept below 2^256 and reduced modulo 2^256 - 38 (twice p), where
  it used ed25519.xo's five 51 bit limbs: products of 16 (or, squared,
  10) word products on adc chains with the high half times 38 folded
  into the low half, sums and differences with a fold of 38 times the
  carry or borrow, the inversion by ref10's fixed chain (254 squarings,
  11 products), and one freeze to the canonical value at the end.
  Ed25519 keeps the 51 bit field (its point formulas add more than they
  multiply). Constant time: no branch or index on a value.

Both generators' output was checked before use by running the
generated code in Python against big integer arithmetic (20,000 random
inputs and the edges near p, 2^255, and 2^256 - 38). TestX25519Diff
(internal/llvm) runs random scalars and points, the base point, and
edge u coordinates (0, 1, p - 1, p, p + 1, 2^255 - 1, the top bit set,
low order points) against RFC 7748 on math/big, itself checked against
crypto/ecdh.

## The arm64 adc chains turned off for correctness (2026-10-16)

The arm64 emitter reloads a template's spilled operands into x16, x17,
then x15 for every further one, so a template with four or more spilled
uses (or two spilled definitions) computes with the wrong value, with
no error (gotchas/arm64-template-scratch-reuse.md). An adc chain was one
template of up to ten uses: correct in every build tested (the new
TestNoSharedScratchReload finds no shared reload in the chained code of
test/codegen/intrinsics or the TLS client), but not by construction.
Until the emitter serves such templates (xo-fa), each adc pair is a
template of its own (at most three uses and two definitions, marked
early and keep, so running out of scratch registers fails the build).
LLVM does not change. M instructions a handshake on arm64:

## Proposal: three compiler intrinsics (approved 2026-10-15, decision 0231)

What stays after steps 1 to 3 is bound by instructions Xo cannot name.

1. **Add and subtract with carry** (`rt.u64_add_carry(a, b, cin) ->
   (Int, Int)` and `rt.u64_sub_borrow`, lowered to `adds`/`adcs` and
   `subs`/`sbcs` on arm64, `llvm.uadd.with.overflow` on LLVM). The
   Montgomery products of nistec.xo and bigmod.xo compute every carry
   with bits.Add64's formula (and, orr, bic, orr, lsr: five instructions
   where arm64 has one flag). Measured with the carries as an unsigned
   compare (a stand-in that is not constant time on every compiler, not
   committed): an X25519 handshake with a P-256 chain 15.9M to 9.9M
   instructions on LLVM, the P-256 group 20.2M to 12.1M (Mbed 5.3M and
   12.9M); the arm64 emitter does not gain without the intrinsic, which
   its flags would serve directly. Go has the same need and solves it
   the same way (bits.Add64 is an intrinsic).
2. **AES rounds in hardware** (`rt.aes_encrypt_round`/`decrypt_round`
   over a 16 byte block, lowered to AESE+AESMC and AESD+AESIMC on arm64
   and AESENC/AESDEC on x86-64, with the bitsliced code as the fallback
   when the CPU lacks them). Bulk AES-128-GCM is 139M instructions a MiB
   on LLVM against Mbed's 14M (the stack's floor), about 110 a byte in
   the bitsliced rounds: constant time in software costs what the
   hardware does in one instruction a round. Hardware AES is constant
   time by design.
3. **Carry-less multiply** (`rt.clmul64(a, b) -> (Int, Int)`, PMULL and
   PMULL2 on arm64, PCLMULQDQ on x86-64) for GHASH, whose constant time
   software multiply (BearSSL's ghash_ctmul64) is the second AES-GCM
   cost.

With 1 the handshakes would be within about 2x of Mbed on LLVM; with 2
and 3 bulk AES-GCM within about 2x (the remaining cost the record copies
both sides make). ChaCha20-Poly1305 (41M a MiB against 14M) would need
vector types for Mbed's NEON speed; no proposal for it now.

## ML-KEM on arm64, the bounds checks (2026-10-16, xo-4)

callgrind (linux/arm64, a CCTV accumulated loop, N=6) by function put
the arm64 build's extra over llvm's in sha3.keccak (7.48M against
5.50M), sha3.read (2.03M against 0.81M), mlkem.ntt (5.85M against
5.11M), ntt_mul_acc and inverse_ntt. The NTT's butterfly was about 30
instructions an element on arm64, two of them bounds checks (cmp and
b.hs) that LLVM proves away.

- mlkem.xo: the NTT, the inverse NTT and ntt_mul_acc read and write the
  coefficients as raw memory (coeffs: a polynomial of N words made
  unique by a first write, as nistec.xo's workspace) in byte offsets (a
  register offset load, no index shift: with element indexes the arm64
  backend added an lsl per access, which cost what the checks saved).
- mlkem.xo sample_ntt reads SHAKE128's output a block (168 bytes) at a
  time, one read rather than seven of 24 bytes (the same stream).
- sha3.xo keccak loads and stores its 25 lanes as raw memory at
  constant offsets.

M instructions per CCTV iteration (a key, an encapsulation, two
decapsulations; (n=21 less n=1) / 20, min of 3, Mac):

| Step | arm64 | llvm |
|---|---|---|
| main 4bf54541-era | 5.11 | 4.19 |
| sample_ntt a block a read | 5.01 | 4.10 |
| the NTT loops raw, element indexes | 4.93 | 3.71 |
| in byte offsets | 4.73 | 3.73 |
| keccak's lanes raw | 4.68 | 3.70 |

crypto_mlkem (CCTV's accumulated digests, Go's vectors, the hybrids),
tls13_go and crypto_prims are unchanged on both backends. The rest of
arm64's gap is the arm64 backend's in Keccak: 362 loads from the stack
and 75 stores a call (spills: its 25 lanes and temporaries), and
`mvn` and `and` where `bic` would do (100 of each); handed to Xo lang 5.
The x25519 handshake of bench/perf/tls (Go's server takes X25519, so
the client makes an ML-KEM share it does not use) is unchanged within
0.3%.
