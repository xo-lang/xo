# netip against Go's net/netip, 2026-10-22

Program: bench/perf/netip/main.xo (200,000 loops: two `parse_addr` of eight
IPv4 and IPv6 texts, `is_private`, two `display`), native arm64 on the
development Mac. Go twin: bench/perf/netip/twin.go.txt (net/netip, go1.26.1).
Instructions retired (`/usr/bin/time -l`).

| | instructions | vs Go |
|---|---|---|
| Xo before (origin/main 781724434) | 1,016 M | 1.85 |
| Xo after | 872 M | 1.58 |
| Go | 551 M | 1.00 |

`xo bench --ab --rounds=9` (B / A, 95% interval): wall 0.829 (0.813 to 0.858),
instructions 0.858 (0.857 to 0.858). Output is the same (md5 of the output).

Steps (instructions, cumulative): `parse_ipv6` by index, no slices of the rest
1,016 M to 936 M; bytes written straight to a builder, no 16 word list 919 M;
displays into one builder, hex group and octet without loops,
`string6` skipping a counted zero run 872 M (a first version cast the ASCII output instead of validating it, 830 M, but the Go backend runs this file and has no cast).

The std/changes figure of 1.7x (2026-10-21) was the Go backend's (99 ms
against Go's 59 ms), not native.

Still above Go: `parse_ipv6`'s per byte loop (44% of the profile), the
`Addr` record (a Bytes of 16 and a zone Str per address, two allocations).
