## 2026-10-29: port: std/rate, golang.org/x/time/rate

- Kind: port
- Commit: this commit
- Found by: gap 15 of the 2026-10-29 std survey (a service limiting its
  clients or its calls out wrote its own token bucket); API approved by
  the lead 2026-10-29 (the Limiter holds its Clock)
- Go reference: golang.org/x/time/rate v0.16.0 rate.go (Limiter,
  reserveN, advance, CancelAt, durationFromTokens, tokensFromDuration)
  and rate_test.go
- Test: internal/stdtest/testdata/corpus/rate, generated by
  scripts/stdcorpus/rate.go from rate_test.go's cases (transcribed, on
  the test's FakeClock), on arm64, LLVM and the Go backend, which
  compiles the native port (std/go_compiled.txt)
- Timing: bench/perf/rate (5,000,000 `allow` calls on 1 token a second, burst 100), quiet --ab, 15 rounds, Xo / Go wall: allow 0.914 (0.911 to 0.92), instructions 1.395; none 0.94

`rate.new(limit, burst, clock)` makes a `Limiter`: `allow`/`allow_n`,
`reserve`/`reserve_n` with `Reservation.delay` and `cancel`, `wait`/
`wait_n` (sleeping on the limiter's clock; cancellation is the task's),
`set_limit`, `set_burst`, `tokens`, and `every(interval)`. Go's `*At`
forms are the clock's time; Go's WaitN context is the task.
