# 0220: Fast builds: parallel whole-program stages, then per-module caching, then per-module compilation

Status: Accepted in stages (owner delegated to the lead, 2026-10-13).
Each stage is gated on the measurement of the one before. Stage 1
implemented (2026-10-14; items 1, 2, and 4 for arm64, item 3 measured,
not the default): the arm64 self build of xo 337.28 s to 59.00 s on the
Mac and 496.82 s to 87.84 s on r8g.8xlarge, output byte identical for 8
and 3 jobs, goldens passing (bench/results/fast-builds-2026-10-13.md
"Stage 1 results"). Stage 2 waits for the lead.

## Context

Xo builds a program as one whole-program compile: the checker over every
module, the SSA builder (which monomorphizes generics on demand, decision
0048), the passes (reference counting with interprocedural borrow
inference, decision 0046; borrowed projections, decision 0186), inlining
(decision 0184), then one backend emitting one assembly file that cc
assembles and links. Most of it runs on one core.

Measured (bench/results/fast-builds-2026-10-13.md, phase 1): the arm64
self build of xo (`xo build --backend=arm64 selfhost/xo`) takes 337 s on
the development Mac and 497 s on r8g.8xlarge. On both, the arm64 emitter
is the largest phase (179 s and 337 s), then cc (112 s and 79 s; `cc -c`
of the 437 MB assembly alone is 47 s on the Mac), then insert_rc (31 s
and 53 s) and inlining (9 s and 19 s). The checker and the SSA builder
together are under 8 s. The emitted program has 6,103 functions and
22.2 million lines of assembly.

Go's make.bash builds three toolchain stages plus std and cmd in 46 s on
the same r8g.8xlarge, keeping about 6 cores busy, by compiling each
package against the export data of its dependencies, in parallel, with a
per package cache (bench/results/go-build-and-test-times-2026-10-13.md).

**Target**: Go's make.bash class: a full self build of xo on r8g.8xlarge
in about 1 to 2 minutes, and seconds for a rebuild after a change to one
module.

## Decision

Three stages, in order. A stage starts only when the measurements of the
previous ones leave the self build well above the target.

**Invariant of every stage**: the output is byte identical whatever the
number of jobs, the order tasks finish in, and the cache's state (the
self rebuild fixed point, s2 and s3 identical, holds; a cached build
equals a fresh one), and the program output goldens pass on arm64 and
LLVM.

### Stage 1: parallelism and cheaper steps inside the whole-program model

Keep one whole-program compile and spend the cores on its per function
stages:

1. **Parallel emission** (arm64): the functions are cut into parts by a
   rule that depends only on the module (contiguous runs of about equal
   SSA size, at least 128 functions each, at most 32 parts), each part
   generated by its own emitter on a task (`XO_EMIT_JOBS`, default the
   cores up to 8), its string, position, and helper labels in a
   namespace of its own. A module of one part is generated exactly as
   before.
2. **Parallel assembly**: each part is a file that assembles apart (its
   functions and the system call trampolines become global symbols of
   hidden visibility), by parallel `cc -c` processes or by selfhost/macho
   on parallel tasks, then one link.
3. **Direct object output**: selfhost/macho assembles in process
   (`--as=macho`), saving the text round trip through cc.
4. **The remaining serial passes**: insert_rc and inlining per function
   on tasks, after the whole-program facts (borrow signatures, the call
   graph) are computed once.
5. **The LLVM backend**: its split build (decision 0168) is the same
   idea at the clang level; its emitter can take the same parts.
   Measured on c7i.8xlarge (2026-10-14): clang is 94 to 97 percent of
   the x86 self build (2,510.80 s at split 12 with 6 jobs, 1,136.45 s at
   32 with 32), about 11,200 s of -O2 CPU on 1.11 GB of IR, so the
   arm64 build's 88 s class needs smaller IR or a lower -O level for
   development builds, not only more jobs.
6. **Development builds of xo at clang -O0** (decided 2026-10-14 by
   the per-change time, bench/results/fast-builds-2026-10-13.md):
   `XO_LLVM_OPT` sets clang's level for the program's IR; `go run
   ./scripts/xobuild` and scripts/bootstrap-xo.sh build xo at -O0 on the
   LLVM path; releases and `xo build --release` stay at -O2. On
   c7i.8xlarge the -O0 xo rebuilds itself in 3:45 against 18:51 at -O2
   (-O1: 17:31) and compiles about 1.5 times slower; the fixed point
   holds within each level.
7. **Smaller output at the source**: one release of a root in place of
   one per heap leaf when all its leaves die together (ssa
   group_releases), and on arm64 a release of a struct of many words
   from memory (mem_drop): arm64 assembly 22.8 to 18.9 million lines,
   calls 4.64 to 1.27 million, LLVM IR 1.12 GB to 313 MB; the arm64
   self build 49.83 s to 34.15 s on r8g.8xlarge, the x86 -O0 self build
   3:45 to 1:23. The releases of a call's results are joined one
   result at a time (ff12d428): a release of the whole call made the
   arm64 emitter load every word of a written back `var` state (5.6
   percent more instructions in the self build). Final (chain L, r8g):
   13.6 million lines, binary 52.0 MB, s2 33.84 s, the compiler's code
   as fast as before (1,260 against 1,259 billion instructions).
8. **Jobs by physical cores and memory**: the emitter's tasks default to
   the physical cores, at most 16 and a third of the memory in GB;
   clang's jobs to the physical cores, at most a quarter of the memory
   in GB.

### Stage 2: per-module caching of front end results

Cache the parse and check of each module, and an export summary of it
(its declarations' types, its effect sets, its generic bodies as checked
trees), keyed by a hash of its sources plus the summaries of its
dependencies. A rebuild after an edit rechecks only the edited module
and the modules whose dependency summaries changed. Low risk: the back
end still sees the whole program, so code is unchanged. The front end is
under 8 s of the 337 s today, so this stage is for edit and check loops
and for stage 3, not for the full build.

### Stage 3: per-module code generation with a link-time merge

Compile each module to an object against the summaries of its
dependencies, in parallel, cached per module, and link at the end, like
Go. The summaries must carry what whole-program passes read today:

- **Monomorphization**: a module instantiates the generic functions it
  uses with its own type arguments; the same instance can appear in
  several modules' objects. Each instance gets a symbol named by its
  generic function and type arguments, emitted as a weak or COMDAT
  definition (Go's dupok symbols), and the link keeps one. The summary of
  a module carries the checked bodies of its exported generic functions.
- **Cross-module inlining**: the summary carries the inline bodies of
  small exported functions (decision 0184's cost model decides which),
  as Go's export data does; a change to such a body invalidates its
  importers' cache entries.
- **Reference counting and borrow inference** (decisions 0046, 0149,
  0186): borrow inference is interprocedural today. Per module, a
  function's borrow signature (which parameters are borrowed, which
  results are fresh) becomes part of its module's summary; calls into a
  module still being compiled in the same strongly connected component
  of the module graph need a fixed point over that component, or a
  conservative default (owned) at the module boundary, measured against
  the whole-program result.
- **Arenas and escape analysis** (decision 0066): the request arena
  audit and the checker's escape rules are per function and per type
  today; what crosses modules (a function storing a parameter, a value
  reaching a task) goes into the summary as a per parameter escape bit.
- **Helpers**: the reference counting, equality, and share helpers per
  type are emitted per object with hidden COMDAT symbols named by the
  type, the link keeping one.

This stage changes the generated code (summary defaults at module
boundaries) and the object layout, so it needs its own measurement of
run time (bench/ three-arm suite) besides build time.

## Consequences

- Stage 1 keeps every whole-program optimization and the output's
  determinism; its ceiling is the slowest part plus the serial phases
  (check, SSA, passes, link): Amdahl's law, measured in
  bench/results/fast-builds-2026-10-13.md.
- Stage 1's parallel output differs from the serial one in label names
  (namespaced per part) and symbol visibility (hidden globals), not in
  code; small programs (one part) are unchanged byte for byte.
- Stages 2 and 3 need a summary format, versioned with the compiler, and
  cache keys that cover the compiler, the target, and the flags.
- Stage 3 makes a one-module rebuild cost one module's compile plus the
  link, which is the only way to reach seconds for an edit of a large
  program.

## Alternatives

- **Per-module compilation first** (stage 3 now): the largest change,
  and it gives up whole-program borrow inference and inlining before
  knowing whether stage 1 suffices. Rejected as the first step.
- **Process level parallelism** (several xo processes, each emitting a
  share of the functions after its own check and SSA): no shared memory
  needed, but each process repeats the front end and the passes (about
  44 s on the Mac) and holds its own copy of the program (about 6 GB);
  the native task runtime already shares the module across threads
  (decision 0058's shared counts).
- **Smaller output instead of faster stages**: the assembly is dominated
  by spill code and reference count calls (5.2 million stack loads and
  1.77 million calls of one drop helper in 22.2 million lines); a better
  register allocator and fewer reference count operations shrink every
  later stage and the binary. Complementary, and tracked separately: it
  changes the code, not the build's structure.
- **An incremental compiler with a query database** (rustc, Salsa): fine
  grained reuse within a module, at the cost of restructuring every pass
  around queries. Stage 2's per module keys get most of the rebuild
  benefit with far less change.

## Addendum (Accepted, 2026-10-14): a per-function back-end cache before stage 2

Status: Accepted (proposed by the fastbuild agent; accepted by the lead,
to whom the owner delegated 0220, 2026-10-14). Built in stages: A, the
objects per part (item 3); B, the per-function assembly cache (items 1
and 2); C, insert_rc per function (item 4), reported on first.

Stage 2 measured (bench/results/fast-builds-2026-10-13.md, "Stage 2
measured"): the front end is 3.3 s of the 37 s self build on the Mac
(check 1.15 s, ssa 2.1 s) and 0.45 s of a 1.2 s service build, so
caching it per module saves at most 9 percent of a self build. After an
edit to one module, the back end still does all the work: insert_rc
7.8 s, inline 2.1 s, emit 15.4 s, assemble 7.5 s on the Mac.

Proposal: cache the back end per function inside the whole-program
model, before stage 2.

1. **Key**: a function's SSA after inlining and reference counting,
   printed canonically (value numbers, types by name), plus what its code
   depends on outside its body: the borrow signatures and by-reference
   flags of its callees, the layouts of the types it touches, the target,
   and the compiler's identity. Equal keys mean equal assembly, because
   the emitter's output for a function depends only on these (its
   labels are numbered per part, which the cache keys by the function's
   index in its part, or the emitter numbers labels per function).
2. **Value**: the function's assembly text and the helpers it names
   (already hashed names, decision 0220 stage 1). On a hit the emitter
   skips isel, the MIR passes, and register allocation for it.
3. **Objects per part**: each part's object is cached by the hash of its
   assembly, so an unchanged part is not assembled again.
4. **Then reference counting per function**: insert_rc's per-function
   work (after borrow inference) is cached the same way, keyed by the
   function's SSA before it and the borrow facts it reads.

Expected after a one-module edit of the compiler: check and ssa (3.3 s)
and borrow inference and inlining (about 3 s) still run; the emit and
assemble work of unchanged functions (most of 23 s) is skipped. Stage 2
(front end per module) then attacks the remaining 3.3 s, and stage 3
the rest.

Consequences: the output stays byte identical (a hit returns what the
emitter would write); the cache costs disk (about the assembly's size
per version kept, 350 MB for the compiler, pruned by age); the key's
completeness is the risk, guarded by a check mode that emits every
function and compares it with the cached text (XO_BACKEND_CACHE=verify).

Alternatives: stage 2 first (smaller gain, measured above); stage 3 per
module code generation (largest gain, largest change).

### Addendum stage A: objects per part (implemented 2026-10-14)

- The parts of a split arm64 assembly are cut by their contents, not by
  equal sizes: a part ends after a function whose name's FNV-1a hash is
  0 modulo 128 once it has 64 functions, or at 512; a module under 256
  functions stays one part (selfhost/arm64 parallel.xo part_bounds). An
  inserted or grown function moves the cuts of its own part at most.
- A part's labels are in a namespace named by the hash of its first
  function's name (`p<6 base 62 digits>_`), its functions numbered from
  0, and call site labels numbered per function, so a part's assembly
  depends only on its own functions (and the program wide facts they
  read). A one-part program is generated as before.
- Each part's object is cached in <user cache>/xo/backend/obj
  ($XO_BACKEND_CACHE_DIR) by the SHA-256 of the assembler's identity
  (cc's `--version` output and file, the compile flags, the source's
  path, which an ELF object names; for selfhost/macho the running xo's
  executable) and the part's text (selfhost/xo asmparts.xo, bcache.xo).
  XO_BACKEND_CACHE=off turns it off. A store prunes, at most once an
  hour, entries unused for a week, then the least recently used past
  XO_BACKEND_CACHE_MAX (8 GiB): selfhost/prune since stage B (decision
  0223; XO_CACHE_MAX_AGE and XO_CACHE_MAX_GB apply).
- Measured (bench/results/fast-builds-2026-10-13.md, "Back-end cache
  stage A"): the self build's assemble phase 6.17 s to 0.75 to 0.90 s
  when every part is reused and 1.49 to 1.53 s after a one-constant or
  one-statement edit (42 of 43 objects reused); user CPU about 130 s to
  about 90 s; the binary with the cache on and off is the same file.

### Addendum stage B: the assembly per function (implemented 2026-10-14)

- In a split generation (a module of 256 or more functions) each
  function is generated as a canonical text: its label prefix
  (`<ns>f<n>`) and Mach-O call site table number are tokens, its
  string literals and fault positions are lists it refers to by index,
  and the helpers it names are a list (selfhost/arm64 fcache.xo). The
  part then places the text: the strings and positions interned in the
  part, the helpers wanted, the program wide flags set, the tokens
  replaced. Placing gives byte for byte what the plain generation
  writes, so the text does not depend on the functions before it in
  its part, and XO_BACKEND_CACHE=off (the plain generation), on, and
  verify give the same binary.
- The key: the SHA-256 of the running xo executable, the target, the
  function's SSA after inlining and reference counting printed in full
  (every field of the function, its blocks, values, and edges), each
  type by name and the hash of every type it reaches (one hash per
  strongly connected component of the type graph, so adding an
  unrelated type changes no key), and the signature of the function
  itself and of every function it names (parameter, result, and
  capture types, `var` parameters, borrowed flags, address taken,
  indirect, entry, export, first of its name). A helper names its type
  or function by name in the entry; a function whose names do not lead
  back to its ids is not cached.
- XO_BACKEND_CACHE=verify generates every function and fails the build
  when an entry differs; it is the test of the key's completeness
  (clean on the self build after a constant, a statement, and a struct
  field edit, on the goldens, and on the fixed point). --release builds
  do not use the per-function cache (the objects' cache still applies,
  keyed by the full text). Default on for every other build.
- Measured (bench/results/fast-builds-2026-10-13.md, "Back-end cache
  stage B"): with every function cached the emit phase of the self
  build is 0.15 to 0.25 s (9.71 s with the cache off), the keys 0.4 to
  1.9 s; after a one-module edit user CPU is 62 to 68 s against about
  90 s with stage A alone and about 140 s without the cache. insert_rc
  (8 to 12 s) is now the largest phase after an edit: stage C (item 4).

### Addendum stage C: insert_rc per function (implemented 2026-10-14)

- insert_rc splits into its whole-program part (the `var` List
  parameters made unique, borrow inference), run every build, and the
  per-function part, cached (selfhost/ssa rccache.xo, rc.xo rc_run) in
  <user cache>/xo/backend/rc for every native backend (lower in
  selfhost/xo), for modules of 256 or more functions, not --release.
- The key: the SHA-256 of the xo executable, the target architecture,
  the function before the pass printed in full with its types by name
  and reach hash (the type keys moved to selfhost/ssa and serve the
  emitter's cache too), its own borrowed flags and whether it is the
  last function of its name, and the borrowed flags of every function
  it names. The entry: the function after the pass, its types by name
  (mapped back to the module's ids on a hit), its opcodes by name. A
  function with a Float constant (no exact text form without float bit
  casts) or a type whose name is not its own is not cached.
- XO_BACKEND_CACHE=verify computes every function and fails on a
  difference; clean on the self build after the three edits, the
  goldens, and the fixed point. off, on, and verify give one binary.
- Measured (bench/results/fast-builds-2026-10-13.md, "Back-end cache
  stage C"): a one-module edit of xo rebuilds in 11.28 to 12.10 s and
  20.42 to 25.30 s of CPU (30.60 s and 131.33 s with the cache off);
  insert_rc 9.25 s to 3.6 s. Left after an edit: check and ssa (3.3 s,
  stage 2), borrow inference and inlining (whole program), the keys,
  the assembly of the changed parts, the link.

### Addendum stage D: the edit loop (implemented in part 2026-10-18)

Status: Accepted (the lead, 2026-10-18: "rebuild only the changed
modules and relink, reusing the back-end function cache and per-module
SSA where safe; the binary identical to a full build").

Measured first (bench/results/fast-builds-2026-10-13.md, "Stage D: the
edit loop"): a one-constant edit of xo rebuilt in 14.1 s and 25.2 s of
CPU with 9,450 of 9,480 functions' reference counting and all but one
function's assembly read from the cache. What ran every build: borrow inference (2.7 s of CPU, a
whole-program fixed point rescanning every function each round), the
serial reads of about 16,000 cache files (1.2 s with other builds on the
machine), inlining (1.9 s) and the verification of the inlined module
(about 1.3 s, every function), the
checker and the SSA builder (3.3 s).

Done (part 1):

1. **Borrow inference by a work list** (compiler/ssa rc.xo
   infer_borrows): after one scan of every function, only a function
   whose flags changed and its callers are scanned again. The flags only
   go from borrowed to owned and each scan is monotone in them, so the
   result is the same greatest fixed point the round robin reached.
2. **Cache entries read on tasks** (compiler/xo bcache.xo
   cache_get_all), for the reference counting and the assembly entries.
3. **The inlined module's verification by key** (bcache.xo
   verify_inlined, compiler/ssa rccache.xo verify_keys, verify.xo
   verify_jobs_skip): a function's verify key is the SHA-256 of its
   emitter key (the function in full after inlining, its types, the
   signatures of the functions it names) and what the verifier reads of
   each function it names besides (the first and last function of the
   name, their borrowed flags, whether they can fault, whether they
   catch, their immortal results). A function whose key verified clean
   in an earlier build is not verified again; the set is one file per
   compiler (<cache>/ver/<identity>), rewritten after a clean
   verification that found new keys. The verification still runs on the
   whole inlined module before dead function removal, so it reports
   what a full verification reports, and the fallback to the module
   without inlining happens in the same builds. XO_BACKEND_CACHE=off and
   verify, --release, and modules the emitter does not split verify
   every function.
4. **fold_tuple_fields** returns at once for a function with no tuple
   (a third of the inliner's time).
5. `--phase-times` prints insert_rc.plan, insert_rc.read,
   insert_rc.apply, inline.apply, and inline.verify.
6. **The test of the invariant**: internal/arm64
   TestBackendCacheEditIdentity builds a program of 300 functions
   through one cache directory first, again, after an edit of one
   constant, after a statement added, and after the revert, and
   requires each binary to be the file XO_BACKEND_CACHE=off builds, with
   functions read from the cache.

Measured (the Mac, main 6fb58769): the warm self build 13.9 s to 9.8 to
10.1 s and 25.1 s to 15.9 s of CPU; a one-constant edit 14.1 s to 11.1
s (CPU 25.2 s to 17.0 s); a statement edit 14.4 s to 11.6 s. insert_rc
5.2 s to 1.7 to 2.1 s, the inlined module's verification about 1.3 s
to 0.5 to 0.8 s. Left after an edit: ssa 2.1 s, inline.apply 1.9 s, check 1.1
s, borrow inference 1.1 s, the decoding of the cached reference
counting 0.5 s, the emitter's keys 0.45 s, emit 0.5 s, assemble and
link 1 s.

Part 2 (design, 2026-10-18), in order of the measured time left:

- **2a, the inliner per function by a chained key** (1.9 s after an
  edit). arm64's inline_module visits the functions in order and copies
  callee bodies as they are at that moment: a callee before the caller
  has been inlined into already, one after it not. So the result for
  function i depends on its own body before inlining, on which names it
  can reach through inlinable callees, and on the version of each such
  callee body: for a callee j < i the result of j, for j > i its body
  before inlining. The key of i is the SHA-256 of:
  - its body before inlining, by its reference counting key (stage C:
    that key determines the pass's output, which is the inliner's
    input);
  - the inliner's settings (XO_PASSES's list, XO_INLINE_DENY);
  - for each name in the closure of its names over the bodies before
    inlining of every inlinable callee (the four sets of
    inlinable_set: small, loopy, padded, few sites): the function's
    index, its membership in each set, whether it is indirect, whether
    it catches, its result count, and the version of its body: the key
    of j for j < i, the reference counting key of j for j > i, "self"
    for j = i.
  Inlining copies names and never makes new ones, so the closure over
  the bodies before inlining holds every name the inlined body can
  reach; the membership bits carry the whole-program facts the sets are
  built from (call site counts, sizes, loops). The keys are computed
  before the pass in function order (each needs the keys before it, not
  the results), so the driver reads every entry at once; a function
  whose reference counting key is "" (not cached, 29 of xo's 9,520), and
  every function whose closure reaches one as an earlier version, is
  not cached. The entry: the function after inlining (ssa's codec of
  stage C) and whether the pass changed it. XO_BACKEND_CACHE=verify
  computes every function and fails on a difference.
  Implemented (2026-10-18, compiler/arm64 inlcache.xo, inline.xo
  inline_state and inline_one, compiler/xo bcache.xo
  inline_through_cache): the hits are decoded first on tasks into a
  list of their own, and the walk in function order puts each in place
  when it reaches it (decoding inside the walk, on one task, took 2.6
  s, more than inlining); the computed functions are encoded on tasks
  after it. The codec had dropped what only the inliner sets (each
  value's inline site, the function's inline sites), so a function read
  back lost its DWARF inline records: found by
  TestBackendCacheModuleFacts under verify, now in the codec (rc cache
  v6), and TestBackendCacheEditIdentity also compares a linux/arm64
  build (an ELF with DWARF; the darwin binary has none), which fails
  without the fix. Measured (the Mac, on c9498eef): a one-constant edit
  of xo 11.1 s to 9.5 s, a statement edit 11.6 s to 9.7 s, the warm
  build 9.8 s to 9.1 s; inlining after an edit 2.75 s to 1.6 s (9,520
  of 9,554 functions read); every cached binary the file the cache-off
  build writes, verify clean after both edits.
- **2a.1, a binary codec for the entries** (implemented 2026-10-18,
  compiler/ssa rccache.xo, rc cache v7): the reference counting and
  inlining entries were text (one line per value, names and types
  spelled out), and decoding them was the largest part of insert_rc.apply
  and inline.apply after an edit. An entry is now the body as varints
  (signed ones zigzag coded) after a header of the codec version and a
  table of the names and types it uses, each written once; the reader
  maps the table to the module's types and ops before the body. After a
  one-constant edit of xo: insert_rc.apply 0.50 to 0.38 s, inline.apply
  1.04 to 0.87 s, user CPU 16.8 to 14.1 s; the wall time stays 9.5 s
  (the decoding ran on tasks beside other work).
- **2b, no decoding of what nothing reads**: a function whose inlined
  result is read from the cache needs its reference counting result
  only as the body a later caller's miss copies; the decoding (0.5 s)
  is done on demand.
  Implemented (2026-10-19, compiler/arm64 inlsum.xo, inline.xo
  inline_walk and inlinable_set, inlcache.xo inline_keys and
  inline_cached, compiler/ssa rccache.xo LazyFn and force_lazy, rc.xo
  rc_cached, compiler/xo bcache.xo rc_cached; the lead's assignment,
  owner-approved). The obstacle was the inliner's whole-program facts:
  the inline keys need the inlinable sets, which inline_walk built from
  every body after reference counting. Those passes now read a summary
  of each body instead (inlsum.xo FnSum: the block count, the adc pairs,
  the counts of constants and of other values that are not calls,
  whether a value has a landing pad and whether a list.set or list.take
  call has one, the calls' names in body order, the names the values
  refer to, indirect, catches, the result count), which inline_state
  builds from the body for a decoded function and reads from the cache
  for an undecoded one, so both cases take one code path. The summary
  is an entry of its own kind (sum/, in the target's pack too) under the
  function's reference counting key, stored when the function is
  computed or decoded without one. With the arm64 and amd64 backends
  (the LLVM and wasm backends read every body and are not lazy), a hit
  whose summary the cache holds is left undecoded: the module holds a
  shell (its name and the facts outside its body) and the entry
  (ssa Module.lazy). inline_cached decodes, on tasks, the functions it
  computes (no inline entry, or verify) and every function in their
  name closures, which is everything inline_one can copy; a function
  read from its inline entry replaces its shell without its reference
  counting ever being decoded. inline_module and the module without
  inlining (when the inlined module does not verify) decode the rest.
  Measured (the Mac, main and the change interleaved, load average 11 to
  20): ssa.decode_func 0.88 s to 0.48 s of CPU in a profile of a
  one-constant edit, inline_state 0.23 s to 0.14 s; insert_rc.apply
  about 0.075 s to 0.02 s, inline.keys 0.29 s to 0.16 s, inline.apply
  0.60 s to 0.40 s; user CPU about 0.5 s less a build; wall 0.2 to 0.7
  s less in six of seven paired builds (one revert was 4 s slower with
  every phase slower, other load). Every cached binary the file of the
  cache-off build, verify clean after both edits;
  TestBackendCacheEditIdentity requires functions left undecoded from
  the third build on, with the loose entries removed.
- **2c, the emitter's key from the chain**: the emitter's and the
  verifier's keys hash the function after inlining again (0.45 s and
  0.4 s); a key over the inlining key and the emitter's own facts (the
  signatures of the functions named, address taken, the target, the
  debug settings) replaces it, so an unchanged function's text is
  placed without its SSA being built at all.
- **2c, first step: the emitter's key from the chain, and float
  constants in the codec** (implemented 2026-10-18, rc cache v8, fn
  cache v6): a function carries the key its body came from (Func.ckey:
  `rc KEY` when insert_rc computed or read it, `inl KEY` when the
  inlining did, cleared by any change after that), and the emitter's key
  hashes that key and the signatures of the functions it names instead
  of the whole inlined text. The codec gains float constants (tag 7, the
  IEEE bits as a signed varint; compiler/ssa floatbits.xo), so a
  function with one is cached instead of recomputed every build: 9,604
  to 9,643 of 9,643 functions read on a warm build of xo. Measured on a
  loaded Mac (fe-g6, on d8d934d4), a one-constant edit of xo:
  insert_rc.apply 0.38 to 0.09 s, fn_keys 0.4 to 0.27 s, user CPU 14.1
  to 10.7 s, wall 9.65 s (load average above 60; the wall time is not
  comparable). Every cached binary the file of the cache-off build,
  verify clean after both edits. The SSA is still built and decoded for
  every function; the rest of 2c (skipping both) needs 2b.
- **2d, packs: one file per kind for a build target** (implemented
  2026-10-19, compiler/xo bcache.xo cache_get_packed, pack_store; the
  lead's assignment, owner-approved). After an edit, reading the entries
  as about 30,000 files (rc, inl, fn) cost about 1.4 s of CPU, nearly all
  of it in the kernel: the opens (1.05 s in a profile of a one-constant
  edit), the used marks' stat and time updates (0.5 s), and the reads.
  Each build target (the working directory, the command line without
  its flags and -o value, and the target) now also keeps its entries in
  segment files, <cache>/pack/<slot>/<kind>/s-<hex>: the format version,
  the count, each entry's key and length, then the entries' bytes. A
  build reads its target's segments (one open each), takes the entries
  it needs, and reads the rest from the loose files; afterwards it
  appends one segment of the entries it used that its pack lacked, and
  past four segments, or a pack holding over twice what it used, writes
  one segment of what it used and removes the segments it read. The
  loose files stay the shared, content-addressed store, written as
  before, so other programs and targets keep their hits, and the entry
  format and keys are unchanged (no cache version bump). A target's
  first build only creates its directory, so a program built once (the
  goldens) costs no second copy; an edit loop's target costs one more
  copy of its entries (about 390 MB for xo: rc 78 MB, inl 95 MB, fn 218
  MB). Segments are written whole and renamed into place; a segment
  removed by another build while this one listed it reads as missing and
  its keys come from the loose files. The prune treats each segment as
  one entry, marked used when read.

  average 50 to 130 from other sessions, so the wall times are not
  comparable): insert_rc.read 0.21 to 0.51 s to 0.03 to 0.11 s,
  inline.read 0.25 to 0.51 s to 0.03 to 0.11 s, system CPU of a build 3.1
  to 4.9 s to 0.9 to 2.5 s, user CPU unchanged (about 11.3 s); in a
  profile of a one-constant edit, _open 1.05 s to 0.06 s and the used
  marks (0.5 s) gone. Every cached binary the file of the cache-off build
  (warm, constant edit, statement edit, revert), verify clean after both
  edits; TestBackendCacheEditIdentity removes the loose entries after the
  second builds, so its edit builds read from the packs alone.
  Lazy decoding (2b, above) came next: the decoding (ssa.decode_func,
  0.8 s of CPU on tasks) was unchanged by the packs.
- **2e, parts** (implemented 2026-10-19, compiler/arm64 partcache.xo,
  parallel.xo gen_part, compiler/xo bcache.xo fn_cache_load and
  fn_cache_store; the lead's assignment, in place of caching each
  function's DWARF, after a measurement: the debug sections were 0.44 s
  of CPU of gen_part's 0.9 s, and every one of xo's 35 parts was
  generated on every build only for the object cache to hash its text,
  34 of 35 hitting). A part (gen_part's result before drop_own_weak,
  which reads the whole module: its functions' placed text, its
  exception tables, its debug sections, its data, the reference flags,
  and the helpers it wants, by name as the fn entries keep them) is an
  entry of the target's pack (kind part; no loose copy: a part is one
  program's, and xo's are 328 MB of text in all) under the SHA-256 of:
  the part cache's version and fn_key's header line (the fn cache
  version, the compiler's identity through the fn keys, the target, the
  architecture, XO_ARM64_REGS, XO_POISON_FRAMES, debug information, data
  addresses in DWARF, nolibc); the compile directory and std's root
  (the debug lines name them); with debug information, the keys of all
  the module's types (a variable's type DIE names its fields and
  variants, which a function's own key may not reach); the number of
  parts, the part's index, its first and last function, and its label
  namespace; and the fn keys of its functions in order. A part is
  generated again when any of its functions' fn keys changes (their
  text, address-taken state, callees' signatures, types: everything
  fn_key covers), when a function is added, removed, or moved before
  or inside it (the split's bounds and the order of the keys), when its
  first function's name changes (its namespace), when the number of
  parts changes, when any type of the module changes with debug
  information on, or when a setting of the header changes; a part with a

  functions in parts it did not read (every function with verify, which
  generates every part and fails the build when one differs from its

  from the fn cache (a part read counts all its functions as reused), so
  a verify build compares the same bytes. A target's parts are cached
  from its third build (the pack is written by the second).

  load average under 16: emit 0.44 to 0.46 s to 0.29 to 0.36 s, user CPU

  generated on tasks, so the CPU saved was mostly parallel). The fn pack
  of xo's target drops from 218 MB to 6 MB (only the entries of parts
  generated are read, so the compaction keeps those). Every cached
  binary the file of the cache-off build, verify clean after both edits.
- **Stage 2** (front end per module) for the checker and the SSA

## Addendum stage 2: the front end per function (design, 2026-10-19)

Status: Accepted (the lead, 2026-10-19: per function, finer than the
per module stage 2 above, which it replaces; diagnostics, constants and
generics, and a verify test added). Not implemented yet.

Measured first (main f33791fc, a warm build of xo under the CPU
profiler, the Mac): 7.3 s of CPU, of which the SSA builder 2.86 s,
serial (function bodies 1.9 s, the lazy check of runtime bodies inside
it 0.51 s, uses_os_tasks 0.30 s), and the checker 1.43 s (function
bodies 1.04 s). After an edit of xo the stage D gate measured the SSA
builder at 2.1 s and the checker at 1.1 s of wall time, the largest
phases left. The SSA builder reads the checker's tables for a body
(each expression's type, call information, resolutions), so the check
of a body can be skipped only when its SSA is not needed or is cached
too: the unit is one function body, checked and built.

### The key

A body's key is checked in two parts, so that it can be computed
before the body is checked:

1. The direct key, SHA-256 of: "xo front cache v1"; the build's flags
   the builder reads (tests, release, real_tests, goos, goarch, wasm,
   nolibc, prof, trace_support, soft_cpu, conc, budgets, imm_rc, the
   checker's native and lazy modes, the std_test path); the body's
   source bytes, its file's path relative to the line root, and its
   first line and column (the debug lines and fault locations); its
   module's path and import table (alias to module path); for a method,
   its receiver's declaration; for a generic function's instance, the
   type arguments by their canonical names.
2. The dependency trace, stored in the entry: every name the body
   resolved outside itself, with what it resolved to (module scope,
   imports, the prelude, methods and fields by receiver type), and for
   each object resolved its interface hash: the SHA-256 of the checked
   declaration (kind, type and effect signature, error type,
   attributes, a function's borrowed and never flags as the checker
   records them, a constant's value, a type's full definition with
   fields, variants and method signatures, transitively for types held
   by value). On a lookup the trace is replayed: each name resolved
   again and each interface hashed again; any difference is a miss.
   A new declaration that shadows a name the body used changes a
   resolution; a changed field or signature changes a hash.

The constants a body folds from other modules (their values) and the
generic instances it requests (callee plus type arguments) are in the
trace and the entry, so an edit of either misses (the lead's (2)).

### The entry

- The diagnostics the body's check produced, exactly (code, span,
  message, notes, fixes; warnings, lints XO05xx, budget findings), and
  the uses it marked (objects referenced, for the unused import and
  unused function diagnostics of other declarations), replayed on a hit
  in the order a cold check produces them (the lead's (1)).
- The checker's effects outside the body that a hit must replay: the
  open type variables it bound (none expected for a function with a
  declared signature; a body that binds one is not cached), and its
  never results.
- When the body was built: its SSA Funcs, closures included, in the rc
  codec's form (types and functions by name); the declares it made
  (callee object by qualified name, type arguments by name), replayed
  into the builder's queue in order; the counters it advanced (closure,
  synthetic type, variable and call site numbers), stored relative and
  rebased on a hit so every later name is the same as in a cold build;
  the builder's side tables it wrote (constants, interfaces, type ids,
  late types, use checks, thunks, natives, the modules it declared
  from, need_conc and uses_net).
- A body the check skipped but the builder needs uncached (a new
  instance, a builder miss) is checked then, as check_lazy does for the
  runtime today.

Counted while building xo (main f33791fc): 8,506 function body jobs
and 269 closure body jobs; 481 bodies request a generic instance (their
type arguments are checker types, which an entry would have to name and
rebuild) and 220 make closures. The first version caches only the
bodies that do neither, about 92% of them; the rest are built as today.

### Order of work

1. The builder side: a hit skips body_of (and check_lazy for a runtime
   body). Measure.
2. The checker side: check_dirs defers the bodies of the root modules
   as lazy mode defers the runtime's, a hit replays the diagnostics and
   uses, a miss checks the body. Measure.
3. Entries in the back-end cache's packs (bcache.xo), one kind ("fe").

### Invariants and tests

- A build with the cache warm, after an edit and after its revert, is
  the same file as a build with XO_BACKEND_CACHE=off (stage D's
  invariant).
- The JSON diagnostics of a warm build (`xo check --json` and `xo build
  --json`) equal a cold build's, for a program with warnings, lints and
  budget findings, before and after an edit (the lead's (1)).
- Verify mode (XO_BACKEND_CACHE=verify) checks and builds every body,
  compares with its entry, and fails on a difference. A test edits a
  type's field, a callee's signature, a constant's value, and adds a
  declaration that shadows a name, and shows that exactly the dependent
  bodies miss (the lead's (3)).
- Wall time per edit reported as for stage D (constant edit, statement
  edit, revert, second edit, warm), with the load average.
